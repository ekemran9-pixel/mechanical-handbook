Hardness testing & PMI

Portable hardness testing, scales and conversions, hardness limits for sour service, and positive material identification.

On this page

  2. Rough conversions (steel)

Scales

ScaleMethodUse
Brinell (HB)Ball indent, measure diameterBase metal, castings, forgings
Vickers (HV)Diamond pyramid, small indentWeld and HAZ surveys (HV10 lab)
Rockwell C (HRC)Diamond cone depthHardened steel, bolts
Leeb (HL)Rebound of an impact bodyPortable, needs mass and thickness, converts to HB/HV

Typical limits

  • Sour service (NACE MR0175/ISO 15156): carbon steel weld and HAZ ≤ 22 HRC (≈ 248 HV10, ≈ 237 HB).
  • NACE SP0472 for refinery wet H₂S: weld metal ≤ 200 HB.
  • P4/P5 after PWHT: often ≤ 225–241 HB.
  • Take readings on weld metal, HAZ both sides and base metal. Prepare surface by grinding to bright metal (remove decarburised layer, ~0.5 mm).

PMI (positive material identification)

  • XRF (handheld X-ray fluorescence): quick, non-destructive. Identifies alloying elements (Cr, Ni, Mo, Mn, Cu…). Cannot measure carbon.
  • OES (optical emission spectroscopy, spark): measures carbon too (L grades, carbon steel carbon content). Leaves a small burn mark.
  • Test every alloy component (pipe, fitting, flange, bolt, weld) per API RP 578 or project spec.
  • Mark tested items with a paint dot or sticker. Log results with item and heat numbers.
  • XRF uses X-rays: operator trained, don't point at people.
