Dye penetrant & magnetic particle testing

Step-by-step PT and MT procedures, materials, indications and common mistakes.

PT (liquid penetrant) procedure

  1. Pre-clean: remove paint, scale, spatter, oil. Wire brushing can smear metal over cracks; use solvent and light grinding. Dry fully.
  2. Apply penetrant (red visible or fluorescent) by spray or brush.
  3. Dwell time: usually 10–30 minutes (per procedure) at 10–52 °C. Keep it wet.
  4. Remove excess: wipe with dry lint-free cloth, then a cloth lightly dampened with remover. Never spray remover directly on the surface (washes penetrant out of the defects).
  5. Apply developer: thin, even white coat from 300 mm. Too thick hides indications.
  6. Inspect: after the development time (often 10–60 min), in good white light (≥ 1000 lux) for visible dye, or UV-A light in darkness for fluorescent.
  7. Record indications, then post-clean.
  • Linear indication (length > 3 × width): crack, lack of fusion, lap.
  • Rounded indication: porosity, pit.
  • False indications: scratches, rough surface, poor cleaning.
  • Use compatible products from the same family (penetrant, remover, developer).
  • Low-halogen and low-sulphur products for stainless and nickel alloys.

MT (magnetic particle) procedure

  1. Clean the surface. Thin paint (< 50 µm) may be allowed per procedure.
  2. Apply white contrast paint (for black ink) if used.
  3. Magnetise with an AC yoke (most common on site). Lift test: AC yoke must lift 4.5 kg (10 lb) at maximum pole spacing.
  4. Apply particles (wet black ink, fluorescent wet, or dry powder) while the field is on.
  5. Inspect while magnetised or just after.
  6. Two directions: repeat with the yoke turned 90°. Flaws parallel to the field don't show.
  7. Check the field with a field indicator (pie gauge, Berthold or flux strips).
  8. Demagnetise if required (e.g. before welding or machining).
  • Pole spacing typically 75–200 mm. Overlap each test area.
  • Prods give strong fields but cause arc burns: not allowed on many pressure parts and machined surfaces.
  • Fluorescent wet method is the most sensitive.
