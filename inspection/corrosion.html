Corrosion types

Recognising the main corrosion and damage mechanisms in plant equipment and what to do about them.

TypeLooks like / wherePrevention
General (uniform)Even thinning, rustCoating, corrosion allowance, inhibitors
PittingDeep small holes. Stainless in chlorides, stagnant waterHigher alloys (Mo, duplex), keep clean, flow
CreviceUnder gaskets, washers, deposits, socket weld gapsAvoid crevices, seal welds, full-face gaskets
GalvanicLess noble metal corrodes next to the more noble (e.g. carbon steel next to stainless or copper in water)Insulating kits, coat the noble metal, same metals
Erosion-corrosionSmooth grooves, horseshoe pits, at bends and after valvesLower velocity, harder materials, long-radius bends
CUI (corrosion under insulation)Carbon steel at −4 to 175 °C under wet insulation; chloride SCC on stainless 50–175 °CCoating under insulation, good cladding, inspection windows
Chloride SCCBranching cracks in austenitic stainless above ~ 50–60 °CDuplex, coatings, keep chlorides away
Caustic crackingCarbon steel in hot causticPWHT, temperature limits
Wet H₂S damage (HIC, SOHIC, SSC)Blisters, stepwise cracking in sour water/gasHIC-resistant plate, hardness control, PWHT
MIC (microbiological)Pits with nodules, firewater and hydrotest water left in linesDrain and dry after hydrotest, biocide
SulfidationThinning in hot sulphur hydrocarbons (> 260 °C)Cr alloy steels, PMI to avoid wrong materials
AtmosphericRust on structures, worst in marine and industrial airPaint systems, galvanising
FatigueCracks at welds, small bore connections, vibrating linesSupports, bracing, good weld profile

API RP 571 describes over 60 damage mechanisms in refineries. Inspection plans (RBI) are built around them.
