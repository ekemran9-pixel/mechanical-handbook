Insulation & heat tracing

Hot and cold insulation materials, cladding details, CUI prevention and electric and steam tracing.

On this page
  1. Materials
  2. Cladding (jacketing)
  3. Cold insulation
  4. Heat tracing

Materials

MaterialRangeNotes
Mineral wool (rock wool)to ~ 650 °CCommon hot insulation. Absorbs water if cladding fails
Calcium silicateto 650 °CRigid, takes foot traffic
Cellular glass (Foamglas)−260 to 430 °CClosed cell, doesn't absorb water. Cold and CUI-sensitive service
PIR / PUR foam−180 to 120 °CCold service with vapour barrier
Aerogel blankets−200 to 650 °CThin, expensive, good for tight spaces
Elastomeric (Armaflex)−50 to 105 °CHVAC, chilled water

Cladding (jacketing)

  • Aluminium (0.8–1.0 mm) or stainless (0.5–0.6 mm, required near fire risk or over stainless pipe in some specs).
  • Overlaps at least 50 mm, arranged to shed water (upper sheet over lower, longitudinal seams at 4 or 8 o'clock, not the top).
  • Seal penetrations (supports, nozzles, instrument tappings) with flashing and sealant.
  • Use screws or bands per spec; screws must not puncture vapour barriers on cold service.

Cold insulation

  • Vapour barrier is critical: any break lets moisture in, it freezes, and insulation fails.
  • Multi-layer with staggered joints.
  • Contraction joints on long runs.

Heat tracing

  • Steam tracing: copper or stainless tube strapped to the pipe, at the bottom of horizontal lines. Each tracer has its own steam trap. Heat transfer cement for better contact on critical lines.
  • Electric tracing: self-regulating cable (most common), constant wattage, or MI cable for high temperatures. Installed straight along the pipe or spiralled, with aluminium tape over it.
  • Megger test electric tracers before and after insulation.
  • Label cladding "Electrically traced".
