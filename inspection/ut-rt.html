Ultrasonic & radiographic testing

How UT and RT work, thickness gauging, film/digital interpretation basics and radiation safety on site.

On this page
  1. Ultrasonic thickness gauging
  2. Weld UT (shear wave)
  3. Radiography basics
  4. Reading radiographs (what defects look like)
  5. Radiation safety on site

Ultrasonic thickness gauging

  • Calibrate on a step block of the same material, covering the range you'll measure.
  • Use couplant (gel, glycerine). Remove loose scale and thick paint, or use echo-to-echo mode through coating.
  • On pipe, use a dual-element probe with the split line across the pipe axis.
  • Take readings at marked CMLs (condition monitoring locations), 4 or more per location (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°), and record.
  • Hot surfaces: high-temperature probes and couplant, apply temperature correction (~1% per 55 °C for steel).

Weld UT (shear wave)

  • Angle probes (45°, 60°, 70°) send sound into the weld. Reflectors echo back.
  • Calibrated on IIW (V1) and V2 blocks for range and sensitivity; reference blocks with side-drilled holes for DAC curves.
  • Scans from both sides of the weld where possible, plus transverse scans for transverse cracks.
  • Indications evaluated by amplitude and length against the code (e.g. ASME VIII, AWS D1.1 tables).

Radiography basics

  • X-ray tubes (shop, up to ~ 40–60 mm steel) or gamma sources: Ir-192 (most site work, 10–60 mm steel), Se-75 (thin, 5–30 mm, better contrast), Co-60 (thick sections).
  • Film or digital (CR/DR) images. Exposure arrangement: single wall single image (SWSI), double wall single image (DWSI), double wall double image (DWDI, small pipe elliptical shot).
  • Image quality indicator (IQI): wire or hole type. The required wire must be visible to prove sensitivity.
  • Film density 2.0–4.0 (ASME V) in the area of interest.

Radiation safety on site

  • RT is usually done at night or in breaks, with the area cleared.
  • Barrier at the controlled area boundary (dose rate commonly 7.5 µSv/h or local limit), with signs, lights and rope. Patrolled.
  • Nobody crosses the barrier until the RT crew says "clear".
  • RT crew uses survey meters and personal dosimeters, and has an emergency procedure for stuck sources.
  • Foreman's job: plan RT windows with the crews and make sure everyone knows the times and boundaries.
