ITP, hold points & QA records

Inspection and test plans, hold and witness points, NCRs, document control and handover packs.

Inspection and Test Plan (ITP)

A table listing each step of the work, the check made, the reference document, the acceptance criteria, the record produced, and who inspects at each step.

CodeMeaning
H (Hold)Work must stop until the named party inspects and releases it
W (Witness)Party is notified and may attend. Work can proceed if they don't come after notice
R (Review)Records are reviewed
S (Surveillance) / M (Monitor)Random checks
P (Perform)Party does the activity

Typical hold points on piping: material receipt, fit-up of critical joints, NDT before PWHT, final dimensional, pressure test.

Notification

Give the inspection notice the ITP requires (often 24–48 h for client witness, longer for third party). Missing a hold point can mean cutting out welds.

Non-conformance (NCR)

  1. Identify and tag the item (red tag, quarantine).
  2. Raise an NCR: what is wrong, against which requirement.
  3. Disposition: use-as-is (needs engineering approval), repair, rework, reject.
  4. Corrective action to stop it recurring.
  5. Close out with evidence.

Records a foreman should keep

  • Daily reports, manpower and equipment.
  • Weld logs and maps, welder ID and continuity.
  • Consumable issue log, oven temperature log.
  • Bolt torque/tension records.
  • Alignment sheets, clearance and runout records (job cards).
  • Test packs and certificates.
  • Calibration records for tools used on QC measurements.

Mechanical completion and handover

  • Walkdown with operations to make a punch list: Cat A (must fix before start-up) and Cat B (can be done after).
  • Mechanical completion certificate when Cat A items are closed and records are complete.
  • Turnover package (MDR): as-built drawings, test packs, material certificates, NDT reports, PWHT charts, vendor data, preservation records.
