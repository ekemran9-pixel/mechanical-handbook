Work orders & CMMS

The work order lifecycle, priorities, writing good notifications and completing work orders properly.

On this page
  1. Work order lifecycle
  2. Priority (typical)
  3. A good notification says
  4. Completing a work order well

Work order lifecycle

  1. Notification / request: someone reports a problem (operator, inspector, foreman).
  2. Approval and priority.
  3. Planning: scope, steps, materials, tools, man-hours, permits, drawings.
  4. Scheduling: assigned to a date and crew.
  5. Execution.
  6. Completion: what was found, what was done, parts used, hours, failure codes.
  7. Technical close: review by planner/engineer, history updated.

Priority (typical)

PriorityMeaningResponse
P1 EmergencySafety, environment, or production stoppedImmediately
P2 UrgentWill fail soon or production reducedWithin 24–72 h
P3 NormalPlanned and scheduledWithin 2–4 weeks
P4 Low / opportunityWhen convenient or at shutdownBacklog

A good notification says

  • Equipment tag (P-101B), not "the pump by the tank".
  • Symptom: what is happening (seal leaking 1 drop per second at the DE).
  • When it started, and anything that changed.
  • Effect on production and safety.
  • Photo if possible.

Completing a work order well

  • As found: condition and readings before work.
  • Cause: what you think caused it (failure code).
  • Action: what was done.
  • As left: final readings.
  • Parts: part numbers and quantities used.
  • Follow-up: anything that still needs doing (new notification).

"Fixed pump" helps nobody. "Replaced DE mechanical seal (cartridge, type xx). Old seal: heat-checked SiC face, flush orifice 50% blocked with scale. Cleaned orifice, aligned to 0.02/0.01. Recommend strainer in Plan 11 line." builds history that stops the next failure.
