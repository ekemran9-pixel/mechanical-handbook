Shutdowns & turnarounds

Turnaround phases, scope freeze, critical path, shift handovers, daily meetings and start-up readiness.

On this page
  1. Phases
  2. Critical path
  3. Daily rhythm
  4. Foreman tips
  5. Start-up readiness

Phases

  1. Scope (12–24 months before): collect work list, challenge each item, freeze scope.
  2. Planning: detailed job packs, materials, contractors, scaffolding, cranes, schedules.
  3. Pre-turnaround: prefabrication, scaffolding, pre-shutdown inspections, mobilisation.
  4. Execution: shutdown, decontamination, isolation, work, inspection, boxing up, testing.
  5. Start-up: de-isolation, leak tests, commissioning.
  6. Post-turnaround: demobilisation, close-out, lessons learned.

Critical path

The longest chain of dependent tasks sets the turnaround duration. Typically: big exchangers, column internals, heater, reactor catalyst change, or major compressor overhaul. Put your best crews and supervision on critical path jobs, with 24-hour cover.

Daily rhythm

  • Shift handover meetings (face to face).
  • Daily progress meeting: critical path status, emergent work, safety.
  • Emergent work (found during inspection) goes through a quick approve-plan-schedule process. Big emergent jobs can push the critical path.

Foreman tips

  • Know your job packs before the turnaround starts.
  • Check that scaffolds, materials and special tools are in place before the equipment is handed over.
  • Inspection hold points: book inspectors in advance.
  • Keep a blind list and a gasket/bolt record for every joint opened.
  • Box up only after inspection sign-off. Photos of internals before closing.
  • Fatigue: 12-hour shifts for weeks. Watch your crew and yourself.

Start-up readiness

  • All blinds removed and checked against the blind list.
  • All joints tagged and torqued, leak tests done.
  • PSVs reinstalled and car-seals in place.
  • Rotating equipment aligned, lubed, coupled, guards on, rotation checked.
  • Housekeeping: scaffolds down, debris removed, drains clear.
