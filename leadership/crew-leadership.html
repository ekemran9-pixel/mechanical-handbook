Leading the crew

Giving instructions, delegation, motivation, handling conflict and discipline, and developing your people.

On this page
  1. Giving instructions
  2. Delegation
  3. Feedback
  4. Conflict
  5. Discipline
  6. Developing people

Giving instructions

Use the five-point brief: What (the task), Why (the purpose), How (key steps, hazards, quality points), Who (who does what), When (deadline and check-in times). Ask them to repeat back the key points.

Delegation

  • Match the job to the person's skill. Stretch a little to develop them.
  • Give authority with the responsibility.
  • Check in at agreed points, not constantly.

Feedback

  • Praise in public, correct in private.
  • Be specific: "The alignment readings you recorded were clear and complete" beats "good job".
  • For problems: describe the behaviour, the effect, and what you need instead. Listen to their side.

Conflict

  • Deal with it early. Talk to each person separately, then together.
  • Focus on the work issue, not personalities.
  • Agree on actions and follow up.

Discipline

  • Know the company procedure. Document facts.
  • Be consistent: the same rule for everyone, including your best worker.
  • Safety violations: stop the work immediately, then follow procedure.

Developing people

  • Keep a skills matrix: who can do what (alignment, seal change, rigging, welding processes, tests).
  • Pair apprentices with skilled fitters. Rotate them through job types.
  • Use this site's quiz for quick checks before letting someone work alone on a task.
  • Recommend courses and certifications (vibration Cat I/II, rigging, laser alignment, welding inspection).
