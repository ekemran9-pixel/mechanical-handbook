Job planning & kitting

Planning a mechanical job: scope, job steps, man-hours, materials, tools, kitting and the job pack.

On this page
  1. Job pack contents
  2. Pre-job walk
  3. Kitting
  4. Scheduling the week

Job pack contents

  • Work order with clear scope.
  • Job steps (method), with hold points.
  • Drawings, isometrics, OEM manual pages, previous history and readings.
  • JSA, permit requirements, isolation list.
  • Bill of materials with reservation numbers.
  • Special tools and equipment (crane, scaffold, hydraulic tools, lasers).
  • Data sheets for recording measurements.
  • Estimated man-hours and crew make-up.

Pre-job walk

Walk the job with the planner before scheduling. Check access, scaffold, lifting points, isolation points, laydown area, obstacles, and whether parts on the drawing match what's in the field.

Kitting

  • Stores pick all parts and consumables into a tagged kit before the job is scheduled.
  • Foreman checks the kit the day before: right part numbers, quantities, sizes, materials, certificates.
  • Missing parts are the most common cause of delays and wrench time loss.

Scheduling the week

  • Load the crew to about 80–90% of available hours. Leave room for breakdowns.
  • Schedule only "ready" work: planned, materials in hand, permits arranged.
  • Group work by area to reduce travel and permit overhead.
  • Agree the schedule with operations (equipment availability).
  • Measure schedule compliance and discuss why jobs slipped.
