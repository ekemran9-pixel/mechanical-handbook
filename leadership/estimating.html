Estimating man-hours

Practical norms for welding (inch-dia), pipe erection, bolt-up, pump overhaul and how to adjust for site conditions.

On this page
  1. Inch-diameter (inch-dia)
  2. Factors
  3. Other norms (rough)
  4. Earned value check

Estimates help plan crews and check progress. Use your site's historical data first. The figures here are rough guides for carbon steel on a normal industrial site.

Inch-diameter (inch-dia)

Welding work is often measured in inch-dia: the pipe NPS × number of joints. A 6" butt weld = 6 inch-dia. Thicker schedules and alloys take longer.

ActivityTypical norm (CS, Sch 40/STD)
Butt weld (field), GTAW root + SMAW0.8–1.2 man-hours per inch-dia (welder + helper)
Butt weld (shop)0.4–0.7 Mh per inch-dia
Socket weld0.5–0.8 Mh per inch-dia
Weld output per welder per shift20–35 inch-dia (field, CS), 40–60 (shop)

Other norms (rough)

  • Pipe erection (handling, setting, aligning), per metre: 0.5–1.0 Mh/m for 2–6", 1.5–3 Mh/m for 8–16".
  • Flange bolt-up (manual, Class 150, 6–8"): 1–2 Mh per joint. Class 600, 12": 3–5 Mh with hydraulic tools.
  • Valve installation (flanged, 6", Class 300): 3–5 Mh.
  • Pump overhaul (OH2, small, workshop): 16–32 Mh.
  • Laser alignment (pump/motor): 2–4 Mh.
  • Mechanical seal change (cartridge, in situ): 6–12 Mh.

Earned value check

Productivity factor = Earned hours ÷ Actual hours

Earned hours = budget hours × % complete. Below 1.0 means behind the norm. Track it weekly for each crew.
