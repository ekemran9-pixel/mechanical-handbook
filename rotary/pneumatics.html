Compressed air & pneumatics

Air treatment, FRL units, actuators, valve symbols, leak management and air quality classes.

On this page
  1. Air treatment
  2. Air quality ISO 8573-1
  3. FRL unit at the machine
  4. Actuators
  5. Valve naming
  6. Leaks
  7. Safety

Air treatment

  • Aftercooler removes most water right after the compressor.
  • Receiver stores air and lets water settle. Drain daily or fit auto drains.
  • Dryers: refrigerated (dew point +3 °C, general use), desiccant (−40 °C or −70 °C, instrument air), membrane (small users).
  • Filters: general (5 µm), coalescing (0.01 µm, removes oil aerosols), activated carbon (oil vapour).

Air quality ISO 8573-1

Written as particles:water:oil, e.g. 1.4.1 for oil-free instrument air, 2.2.2 for instrument air with desiccant dryer. Instrument air typically needs a pressure dew point at least 10 °C below the lowest ambient temperature.

FRL unit at the machine

  • Filter: drains water, catches dirt. Check and drain bowl.
  • Regulator: sets working pressure. Set the lowest pressure that does the job.
  • Lubricator: only where components need it. Most modern valves and cylinders are pre-lubed for life; adding oil then washes the grease out and you must keep oiling.

Actuators

  • Single acting (spring return) and double acting cylinders.
  • Rotary actuators (rack and pinion, scotch yoke) on quarter-turn valves. Spring return is fail-safe.
  • Cushioning screws at the cylinder ends set the end-of-stroke deceleration.

Valve naming

Ports/positions: a 5/2 valve has 5 ports and 2 positions (standard for double-acting cylinders). A 3/2 valve has 3 ports and 2 positions (single acting cylinders, pilot signals). Port numbers (ISO 5599): 1 supply, 2 and 4 outlets, 3 and 5 exhaust, 12/14 pilots.

Leaks

Leaks are typically 20–30% of compressor output on an unmanaged site. A 3 mm hole at 7 bar wastes roughly 10 L/s, around 3 kW of compressor power, all year.

  1. Survey with an ultrasonic detector during production, and again at shutdown (you can hear leaks on a quiet weekend).
  2. Tag each leak with a number and log it.
  3. Fix fittings, hoses, quick couplers, FRL drains, and cylinder rod seals.
  4. Re-survey every 6 months.

Safety

  • Never use compressed air to clean clothes or skin. Air injection kills.
  • Use blow guns limited to 2 bar at the nozzle (OSHA 30 psi) with chip guard.
  • Whip restraints on hoses above 12 mm or where a coupling could fail.
  • Vent to zero before breaking any connection.
