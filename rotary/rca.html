Root cause analysis

A practical RCA method for equipment failures: 5 Whys, fishbone, fault tree and physical evidence.

On this page
  1. When to do an RCA
  2. Preserve the evidence
  3. The 5 Whys
  4. Fishbone (Ishikawa)
  5. Physical, human and latent roots
  6. Report template

Repairing the same pump four times a year is expensive. RCA finds why it fails so you can stop it.

When to do an RCA

  • Any safety or environmental event.
  • Repeat failures (e.g. the same machine twice in 12 months).
  • High-cost failures or long downtime.
  • Bad actor list: the top 10 machines by maintenance cost.

Preserve the evidence

  1. Photograph the machine before and during dismantling.
  2. Take oil samples before draining.
  3. Record as-found measurements: alignment, clearances, positions, bolt tightness.
  4. Bag and tag failed parts (bearings, seal faces, gaskets). Don't clean them.
  5. Collect data: operating trends (flow, pressure, temperature, vibration) around the failure time, work order history, operator logs.

The 5 Whys

Ask "why?" until you reach something you can fix with a system change.

  1. Why did the pump stop? Bearing seized.
  2. Why did the bearing seize? No oil in the housing.
  3. Why was there no oil? The constant level oiler was empty.
  4. Why was it empty? It isn't on the lube route.
  5. Why isn't it on the route? The pump was installed last year and the route was never updated.

Root cause: no process to add new equipment to lube routes. Fix: add an MOC/handover step that updates the PM and lube routes.

Fishbone (Ishikawa)

List possible causes on branches: Machine, Method, Material, Man (people), Measurement, Environment. Then test each one against the evidence. Cross out the ones the evidence disproves.

Physical, human and latent roots

  • Physical root: the mechanism (fatigue, corrosion, overload, wear).
  • Human root: the decision or action that caused it (wrong oil added).
  • Latent root: the system or organisational gap that allowed it (no labelling, no training).

Fixing only the physical root means it will happen again somewhere else.

Report template

  1. What happened (one paragraph, facts only).
  2. Impact (safety, production, cost).
  3. Timeline.
  4. Evidence (photos, measurements, data).
  5. Analysis (5 Whys, fishbone).
  6. Root causes (physical, human, latent).
  7. Actions: owner, due date, how we'll know it worked.
Blame-free. People hide facts when they fear punishment. Focus on how the system let it happen.
