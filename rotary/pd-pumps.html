Positive displacement pumps

Gear, screw, progressive cavity, lobe, vane, diaphragm and reciprocating plunger pumps: how they work and how to maintain them.

On this page
  1. Rotary PD types
  2. Gear pump checks
  3. Progressive cavity pumps
  4. Diaphragm pumps
  5. Reciprocating plunger pumps
  6. Metering pump calibration

A positive displacement (PD) pump traps a fixed volume and pushes it out. Flow stays almost constant whatever the pressure. Pressure rises until the motor stalls or something breaks if the discharge is blocked.

Always fit a relief valve on a PD pump discharge, set below the weakest component rating, and never start against a closed discharge valve.

Gear pump checks

  • End clearance (gear to side plate) controls slip more than tooth clearance. Typical total 0.05–0.13 mm. Measure with plastigage or by gasket thickness.
  • Bushings worn oval allow gears to touch the casing. Look for a bright arc on the casing bore.
  • Relief valve: test and set on the bench.
  • Rotation: gear pumps are often direction-specific. Check the arrow.

Progressive cavity pumps

  • Never run dry. The stator burns in seconds.
  • Stator swell from wrong elastomer causes high start torque and overload.
  • Check pin joints or gear joints and their seals at each service.
  • Flow drops as the stator wears. Measure output against new.

Diaphragm pumps

  • Air-operated double diaphragm (AODD): no motor, can run dry, self-priming. Common for drainage and chemical transfer. Freezing at the air exhaust stops them; use a dry air supply.
  • Check diaphragms, ball checks and seats. A torn diaphragm sends product out the air exhaust.
  • Hydraulic diaphragm metering pumps: the plunger moves oil, oil flexes the diaphragm. Keep the oil topped and bled of air, or the stroke shortens.

Reciprocating plunger pumps

  • Used for high pressure: injection, glycol, chemical dosing, hydrotest pumps.
  • Flow pulses with each stroke. Fit pulsation dampeners (nitrogen pre-charge typically 60–80% of operating pressure).
  • Suction valves, discharge valves and plunger packing are the wear parts.
  • Adjust packing to a slight weep where the design allows. Over-tight packing scores the plunger.
  • Check crosshead and connecting rod bearings like a small engine.
  • Suction needs good NPSHa. Acceleration head adds to the requirement, so keep suction lines short and large.

Metering pump calibration

  1. Fill a calibration column (graduated cylinder on the suction).
  2. Close the tank supply so the pump draws from the column only.
  3. Time how much it draws in 60 s at the set stroke.
  4. Adjust stroke length or speed. Repeat at 3 settings and draw the calibration line.
