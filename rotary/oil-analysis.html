Oil analysis

Taking good samples and reading viscosity, water, particle count, wear metals, TAN and oxidation results.

On this page
  1. Taking a good sample
  2. Key tests
  3. Wear metals: where they come from
  4. ISO 4406 cleanliness code
  5. Acting on a report

Taking a good sample

  • Take it from the same point every time, while the machine runs at normal temperature.
  • Best point: a sample valve on the return line before the filter, or a live zone in the sump. Never from the drain at the bottom.
  • Flush the valve and line first (5–10 times the dead volume).
  • Use clean bottles. Fill to about 80%. Label right away: machine, date, hours, oil type.
  • Send it within 24 hours.

Key tests

TestTells youTypical alarm
Viscosity @ 40 °CWrong oil added, oxidation, fuel or solvent dilution, shear±10% caution, ±20% critical

Particle count (ISO 4406)Dirt, wear debris, filter performanceHydraulics 17/15/12 or cleaner; turbines 16/14/11

Wear metals: where they come from

ElementPossible sources
Iron (Fe)Gears, shafts, bearings, cylinder liners, rust
Chromium (Cr)Bearing steel, chrome-plated rods, rings
Copper (Cu)Bronze bushings, cages, cooler tubes (leak or corrosion), thrust washers
Tin (Sn)Babbitt bearings, bronze
Lead (Pb)Babbitt (lead based), bearing overlays
Aluminium (Al)Pistons, housings, thrust washers. With silicon: dirt
Silicon (Si)Dirt and dust, or silicone antifoam additive
Sodium (Na), Potassium (K)Coolant or seawater leak
Zinc, Phosphorus, CalciumAdditives. A change means wrong oil top-up

ISO 4406 cleanliness code

Three numbers for particles ≥ 4, ≥ 6 and ≥ 14 µm per mL. Each step up of 1 doubles the count. So 18/16/13 has twice the particles of 17/15/12. Cleaning oil by two codes can roughly double component life in hydraulics.

Acting on a report

  1. Read the trend, not just this sample.
  2. Caution: resample within 2 weeks and check the obvious (breather, seals, top-up records).
  3. Critical: inspect the machine, check vibration and temperature, plan an outage.
  4. Record what you found when you opened it. Feed it back to the lab or analyst.
