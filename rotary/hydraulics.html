Hydraulic systems

Hydraulic components, circuit symbols, fluid cleanliness, accumulators, troubleshooting and safe maintenance.

On this page

  2. Cleanliness

Parts of a system

ComponentJob
ReservoirStores oil, lets air and dirt settle, cools. Size about 3× pump flow per minute
Pump (gear, vane, piston)Makes flow. Pressure comes from resistance
Relief valveLimits maximum pressure
Directional control valveSends flow to the actuators. Solenoid, manual, pilot or proportional
Flow control valveSets speed
Check valve, pilot-operated checkOne-way flow, load holding
Counterbalance valveControls overrunning loads (cranes, lifts)
CylinderLinear motion. Force = pressure × area
MotorRotary motion
AccumulatorStores energy, damps pulses, emergency power
FiltersSuction strainer, pressure filter, return filter, breather
CoolerKeeps oil 40–55 °C
Force (kN) = Pressure (bar) × Area (cm²) ÷ 100
Speed (m/min) = Flow (L/min) ÷ (Area (cm²) × 0.1)

Example: 100 mm bore cylinder (78.5 cm²) at 160 bar = 125.6 kN push.

Cleanliness

About 70–80% of hydraulic failures come from contaminated fluid. Servo and proportional valves need ISO 4406 16/14/11 or cleaner; general systems 18/16/13; piston pumps 17/15/12.

  • Filter new oil when filling. New oil is often dirtier than the system needs.
  • Use desiccant breathers.
  • Cap every open hose and port immediately.
  • Change filter elements on ΔP indicator, not only by hours.

Accumulators

  • Bladder, piston or diaphragm type, pre-charged with dry nitrogen only. Never oxygen or air.
  • Pre-charge typically 0.9 × minimum system pressure (energy storage) or 0.6–0.8 × working pressure (pulsation).
  • Check pre-charge with the oil side at zero pressure, using a charging kit.
Stored energy. Before working on any hydraulic circuit: stop the pump, lower or block loads, dump accumulators to tank, and check gauges read zero. A pin-hole leak at 200 bar can inject oil through skin. That is a surgical emergency.

Hoses and fittings

  • Hoses: SAE 100R2 / EN 853 2SN (two wire braid) common for medium pressure; 4SP/4SH spiral for high pressure.
  • Minimum bend radius from the hose table. No twist after fitting (watch the lay line).
  • Common threads: BSPP (parallel, with bonded seal), BSPT, NPT, JIC 37° flare, ORFS (O-ring face seal, best for leaks), metric DIN 24° cone (DKO).
  • Never mix JIC and 45° SAE flare. They look similar but leak.
  • Replace hoses at the age limit (often 5–6 years) or on damage.

Troubleshooting

SymptomCheck
No pressurePump not turning, coupling broken, relief valve stuck open, oil level low, wrong valve position
Slow actuatorPump wear (check case drain flow on piston pumps), internal leakage, flow control setting, relief setting low
Noisy pumpCavitation (blocked suction strainer, cold oil, high suction lift), aeration (air leak on suction, low oil level)
Oil overheatingRelief valve dumping continuously, cooler fouled, internal leakage, wrong viscosity
Cylinder driftsPiston seal leak, valve spool leakage, pilot check leaking
Erratic motionAir in system, sticking valve, dirt

Cylinder bypass test: extend fully, remove the rod-side hose (capped on the valve side), pressurise the cap side. Oil coming out the rod port means the piston seal leaks.
