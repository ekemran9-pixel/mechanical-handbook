Belt conveyors

Belt tracking, idlers, pulleys, take-ups, splices, scrapers and safe working around conveyors.

On this page
  1. Parts
  2. Belt tracking
  3. Splices
  4. Inspection

Parts

  • Drive pulley (lagged), tail pulley, bend and snub pulleys.
  • Carrying idlers (troughed), return idlers, impact idlers at the loading point, training (self-aligning) idlers.
  • Take-up: gravity or screw. Keeps belt tension.
  • Belt cleaners (primary and secondary scrapers), skirts and seals.
  • Pull-wire emergency stops, belt sway switches, speed sensors.

Belt tracking

A belt moves toward the end of the idler it touches first. It steers away from the high side.

  1. Check the structure is square and level, and the belt splice is square.
  2. Check all idlers are square to the belt and turning.
  3. Start at the head end and work along the return side, then the carrying side.
  4. Move the idler end forward (in the direction of belt travel) on the side the belt runs toward. Small moves, one idler at a time.
  5. Use pulleys for tracking only as a last resort.
  6. Clean build-up on pulleys and idlers. Build-up is a major cause of mistracking.

Splices

  • Hot vulcanised: strongest, longest life. Needs a press and trained crew.
  • Cold vulcanised: adhesive, good for repairs.
  • Mechanical fasteners: quick, lower strength, wear scrapers.
  • Step lengths and bias angle per the belt maker.

Inspection

  • Seized idlers (wear flats on the belt, fire risk).
  • Pulley lagging wear, bearing temperatures.
  • Belt edge damage, cover wear, rips.
  • Scraper blade tension and wear.
  • Take-up travel remaining.
Conveyors kill by entanglement. Never clean or adjust a moving conveyor. Lock out the drive and the take-up energy (a gravity take-up can move the belt). Guards on all nip points.
