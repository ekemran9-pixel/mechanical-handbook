Agitators & mixers

Top-entry and side-entry agitators, gearboxes, shaft runout, steady bearings and agitator seals.

  • Top-entry: motor and gearbox on a vessel nozzle, long overhung shaft, impellers (pitched blade, hydrofoil, Rushton turbine).
  • Side-entry: large storage tanks. Seal replacement often possible with the tank full using a shut-off device.
  • Bottom-entry: mixing at low level. Seal in contact with the product.

Checks

  • Shaft runout at the seal: typically ≤ 0.1 mm for mechanical seals; check after impeller fitting.
  • Shaft runout at the bottom end: check against OEM (long shafts amplify small errors at the gearbox flange).
  • Steady bearing (bottom bush) wear if fitted.
  • Impeller blade angle and bolting. Use thread locker and lock wires.
  • Gearbox oil and dry-well (no oil leak into the vessel).
  • Mounting flange level and bolt torque.

Running

  • Don't run with the impeller near the liquid surface or above it (unsteady loads) unless designed for it.
  • Check rotation: some impellers pump down and must turn one way.
  • Agitator seals often use a dual seal with barrier pot (Plan 53A) for reactors.
Vessel entry. An agitator is a major hazard during confined space entry. Lock out the motor electrically and, if possible, mechanically restrain or uncouple the shaft.
