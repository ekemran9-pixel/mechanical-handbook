Reciprocating compressors

Frame, cylinder and valve maintenance, clearances, rod runout, packing, rider bands and the start-up checks.

On this page

  2. Valves

  4. Piston rod runout

Main parts

  • Frame: crankcase, crankshaft, main bearings, connecting rods.
  • Crosshead: converts the rod's rocking motion to straight motion. Runs on guides.
  • Distance piece: space between frame and cylinder, separating oil from gas. Single or double compartment.
  • Piston rod packing: rings in a packing case seal gas along the rod.
  • Cylinder: liner, piston, piston rings, rider (wear) bands.
  • Valves: suction and discharge, plate, ring, poppet or channel type.
  • Unloaders: finger unloaders, plug unloaders or clearance pockets for capacity control.

Valves

Valves cause around 40% of recip compressor unplanned stops.

  • Suction and discharge valves look alike but install opposite ways. Most designs physically prevent wrong fitting; always check the arrow or the "S"/"D" marking.
  • Check plates and rings for cracks, chips, wear. Springs for broken coils and set height.
  • Lap seats flat. Replace plates rather than reusing cracked ones.
  • Use new gaskets on valve seats. Tighten valve cover bolts in a cross pattern to torque.
  • Leaky valves show as high valve cover temperature (thermography or contact probes). A hot suction valve cover is a classic sign.

Piston rod runout

Mount a dial on the frame near the packing, touching the rod top (vertical) and side (horizontal). Bar the machine over a full stroke.

  • Typical limit: vertical and horizontal ≤ 0.0015 mm per mm of stroke (API 618: about 0.0006 in per in of stroke).
  • Large vertical runout: worn crosshead shoes, worn rider bands, misaligned cylinder.
  • Large horizontal runout: piston not square on the rod, crosshead guide misalignment, bent rod.

Rod drop

As rider bands wear, the piston sinks and the rod drops at the packing. A proximity probe under the rod at the packing case measures it online. Set an alarm at about 50–60% of allowable band wear.

Packing

  • Packing rings: radial cut and tangential cut pairs, with a backup ring. Fit in the right order and direction (the side marked to the pressure).
  • Vent and purge connections must be clear. Rising vent flow means packing wear.
  • Check the rod finish where it runs in the packing: polish out scratches, hard-coated rods must not be ground.

Start-up checks

  1. Lube oil pump on, pressure normal. Cylinder lubricator primed and feeding (check each point).
  2. Cooling water flowing through jackets.
  3. Bar over at least 2 full turns by hand or barring device. Feel for liquid lock or rubbing.
  4. Drain knockout drums and suction scrubbers.
  5. Start unloaded. Load in steps once temperatures are stable.
  6. Check valve cover temperatures, rod packing temperature and vibration.
Liquid in the cylinder breaks rods and heads. Check scrubbers, levels and drains every shift on wet gas service.
