Pump overhaul procedure

Step-by-step strip, inspect, measure and rebuild of an OH2 process pump, with the readings to record.

On this page
  1. Before you start
  2. Strip
  3. Inspect and measure
  4. Rebuild
  5. Record sheet

This sequence fits a typical OH2 back pull-out pump with a cartridge seal and oil-lubricated bearings. Adapt it to the maker's manual.

Before you start

  1. Permit, LOTO on the motor breaker, suction and discharge valves locked shut. Flush and seal lines isolated.
  2. Drain and vent the casing to a safe place. Check for zero pressure on the gauge.
  3. Gas test if the product is flammable or toxic.
  4. Take as-found readings: alignment, coupling gap, vibration history, seal leakage note.
  5. Get spares ready: seal, bearings, gaskets, O-rings, wear rings if due, lock washer and nut.

Strip

  1. Remove coupling guard and spacer. Mark the hubs.
  2. Drain the bearing housing oil. Keep a sample for analysis.
  3. Disconnect seal flush and cooling lines. Cap the open ends.
  4. Fit the seal setting clips on the cartridge seal before loosening anything.
  5. Support the bearing frame with a sling or stand. Remove the casing bolts. Use jacking screws, never wedges between faces.
  6. Pull out the rotating assembly. Take it to a clean bench.
  7. Remove the impeller nut (usually left-hand thread on a clockwise pump, check). Remove the impeller with a puller if tight.
  8. Slide off the seal cartridge. Remove the coupling hub with a puller or heat.
  9. Remove bearing end covers and press out the shaft and bearings.

Rebuild

  1. Clean all parts. Blow out oil passages.
  2. Heat bearings on an induction heater to about 110 °C. Fit against the shoulder and hold until it grips.
  3. Fit angular contact pairs in the right arrangement (usually back-to-back, the "V" points outward on the outer rings' marks).
  4. Fit the lock washer and nut once the bearings cool. Tighten, then bend a tab.
  5. Fit the shaft into the housing, then the end covers. Check shaft end float with a dial: typical 0.05–0.10 mm for paired angular contact bearings, larger for ball/roller combos.
  6. Check shaft runout at the seal sleeve and the impeller fit, mounted in its own bearings.
  7. Check seal chamber face runout with the dial on the shaft: ≤ 0.013 mm per 25 mm of shaft diameter.
  8. Fit the seal cartridge and impeller. Torque the impeller nut to OEM value.
  9. Fit the new casing gasket. Install the rotating assembly. Tighten casing bolts evenly in a star pattern.
  10. Check the rotor turns freely by hand. For semi-open impellers set front clearance now.
  11. Tighten the seal gland nuts, then remove setting clips.
  12. Refit the coupling hub, align the pump (see Shaft alignment).
  13. Fill bearing housing oil to the mark. Reconnect flush lines.

Record sheet

Record at least: shaft runout (3 points), end float, ring clearances (old and new), bearing numbers fitted, seal serial number, impeller nut torque, final alignment readings, soft foot readings, coupling gap. Sign and date it. These numbers are the starting point for the next failure analysis.

Tip. Photograph each part as removed. A failed seal face or bearing tells you why it failed. Keep them for the RCA, don't throw them in the scrap bin.
