Pump types & API 610 codes

OH, BB and VS pump families, multistage, vertical turbine and submersible pumps, and where each is used.

On this page
  1. Overhung (OH)
  2. Between bearings (BB)
  3. Vertically suspended (VS)
  4. Vertical turbine pumps
  5. Multistage pumps
  6. Submersible pumps
  7. Quick selection guide

API 610 sorts centrifugal pumps by how the impeller is carried. Knowing the code tells you how the pump comes apart.

Overhung (OH)

CodeDescriptionNotes
OH1Foot-mounted, flexibly coupledLight duty ANSI pumps. Not usually API
OH2Centreline-mounted, flexibly coupled, own bearing housingThe standard refinery process pump. Back pull-out design
OH3Vertical in-line, separate bearing bracketSaves space, bolts into the pipe
OH4Vertical in-line, rigidly coupled
OH5Vertical in-line, close-coupled (impeller on motor shaft)Motor bearings carry pump load
OH6High-speed integral gearSundyne type. Low flow, very high head

Back pull-out: with a spacer coupling you remove the spacer and lift out the bearing frame, shaft, seal and impeller as one unit. The casing stays bolted to the pipe. Alignment of the motor stays untouched.

Between bearings (BB)

CodeDescriptionTypical use
BB1Axially split, 1–2 stageLarge water, cooling water, pipeline
BB2Radially split, 1–2 stageHot hydrocarbon, high pressure
BB3Axially split, multistagePipeline, water injection, boiler feed
BB4Radially split, single casing multistage (ring section)Boiler feed (medium duty)
BB5Radially split, double casing (barrel)High pressure boiler feed, water injection. Cartridge bundle pulled as one

Vertically suspended (VS)

CodeDescriptionTypical use
VS1Wet pit, diffuser, discharge through columnVertical turbine pumps for cooling water, river intakes
VS2Wet pit, volute, discharge through column
VS4Line shaft, discharge separateSump pumps
VS5Cantilever (no bearings below)Sump pumps, dirty fluids
VS6Double casing diffuser (can pump)Low NPSHa: LPG, condensate
VS7Double casing volute

Vertical turbine pumps

  • Line shaft bearings: water-lubricated rubber or product-lubricated. Check each for wear at overhaul.
  • Impeller lift set by the top adjusting nut. Lift = shaft stretch under thrust + running clearance. Too little and the impellers drag on the bowls. Follow the maker's lift.
  • Column joints must be tight and square. Use thread compound on threaded columns.
  • Check rotation before coupling. Reverse running unscrews threaded shafts.
  • Non-reverse ratchet in the motor stops backspin on shutdown.

Multistage pumps

  • Several impellers in series on one shaft. Head adds stage by stage.
  • Thrust balance: balance drum, balance disc, or opposed impellers (back-to-back).
  • Assemble stages in the right order. Mark each diffuser and impeller on dismantling.
  • Check total rotor float and the half-float position before fitting the thrust bearing.
  • Balance line from the balance chamber to suction must be clear. A blocked line overloads the thrust bearing quickly.

Submersible pumps

  • Motor and pump in one sealed unit under the liquid. Common in sumps, wells and drainage.
  • Check motor insulation (megger) and the oil chamber between seals at each service. Water in the oil means the lower seal is leaking.
  • Check the cable entry. Never lift by the cable.

Large flow, low head, waterBB1 split case or VS1
High pressure boiler feedBB4 or BB5
Very low NPSHaVS6 can pump or inducer
