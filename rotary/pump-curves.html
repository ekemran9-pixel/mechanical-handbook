Pump curves & system curves

Read a pump curve, find the operating point, use affinity laws and understand pumps in parallel and series.

On this page
  1. What the curve shows
  2. System curve
  3. Affinity laws
  4. Pumps in parallel
  5. Pumps in series

  7. Reading the field data

What the curve shows

A pump performance curve plots, against flow (Q):

  • Head (H): falls as flow rises. The point at zero flow is shut-off head.
  • Efficiency (η): rises to a peak at the best efficiency point (BEP), then falls.
  • Power (P): brake power, usually rises with flow for radial impellers.
  • NPSHr: rises with flow, steeply at high flow.

Curves are often drawn for several impeller diameters. The rated point is marked on the data sheet.

System curve

H_system = H_static + k × Q²

Static head is the elevation difference plus the pressure difference between the two tanks. Friction rises with the square of flow. The pump settles where its curve crosses the system curve.

  • Throttling discharge: adds friction, steeper system curve, less flow. Wastes energy but is simple.
  • Clogged strainer or fouled exchanger: same effect as throttling.
  • Higher destination tank pressure: raises static head, moves the whole curve up.

Affinity laws

For the same pump with a speed change (N), or a small impeller trim (D, up to about 10%):

Q2 = Q1 × (N2/N1) H2 = H1 × (N2/N1)² P2 = P1 × (N2/N1)³

Cut speed by 20% and power drops to about 51%. That's why VFDs save so much energy on pumps with mainly friction head. With high static head the savings are smaller, because the pump must still overcome the static lift.

NPSHr changes roughly with speed squared too.

Pumps in parallel

  • Heads stay the same, flows add at each head.
  • The second pump adds much less than double the flow if the system curve is steep.
  • Pumps must have similar shut-off heads. A weaker pump can be pushed back to shut-off and overheat.
  • Each pump needs a check valve on the discharge.

Pumps in series

  • Flows stay the same, heads add.
  • The second pump's casing must be rated for the combined pressure.
  • Used for booster and transfer duties with high static head.

Specific speed

Ns = N × √Q ÷ H^0.75 (rpm, m³/s, m)

Low Ns (below about 30 metric) means a narrow radial impeller, high head and low flow. High Ns means a mixed-flow or axial impeller, low head and high flow. Suction specific speed (Nss, using NPSHr) above about 11,000 US units (213 metric) warns of a narrow stable operating range.

Reading the field data

  1. Read suction and discharge pressure gauges. Convert to head with SG.
  2. Differential head = (Pd − Ps) × 10.2 ÷ SG, plus the gauge height difference.
  3. Read flow from the flowmeter, or estimate from tank level change.
  4. Plot on the curve. Below the curve at the same flow means internal wear (rings, impeller) or wrong speed.
  5. Check amps against the power curve for SG and viscosity.
Field rule. A pump making 10% less head than its curve at the same flow usually has worn wear rings, worn impeller or is turning slow. Check speed first, then open it up.
