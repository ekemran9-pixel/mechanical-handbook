Belt & chain drives

V-belts, wedge belts, timing belts and roller chains: alignment, tensioning, inspection and replacement.

V-belt sections

SectionTop width (mm)Notes

3V, 5V, 8V9.5, 16, 25.4American narrow sections

SP sections are wedge (narrow) belts carrying more power than classical belts of the same width. Never mix sections or brands on one drive.

Pulley alignment

  • Use a laser pulley tool or a straight edge across both pulley faces.
  • Check parallel offset and angular (both vertical and horizontal) misalignment.
  • Maximum: about 0.5° angular for V-belts, 0.25° for timing belts. Offset ≤ 1 mm per 100 mm of centre distance (V-belts).
  • Pulleys with different face widths: measure from the groove centre, not the face.

Tensioning V-belts

  1. Measure the span length (between the contact points).
  2. Deflection = 16 mm per 1,000 mm of span (1/64 inch per inch).
  3. Press the centre of the span with a tension gauge until it deflects that amount.
  4. Compare the force with the belt maker's table for the section and small pulley size.
  5. Run 30 min to 24 h, then re-tension (new belts stretch most in the first hours).

Sonic tension meters measure the belt's natural frequency, which is more accurate. Enter belt mass and span.

Signs of too low tension: squeal at start, slip, glazed sides, heat. Too high: bearing overload, short belt and bearing life.

Inspecting belts and pulleys

  • Cracks on the underside, glazing, swelling (oil), uneven wear.
  • Belt riding low in the groove: worn groove or worn belt. Use a groove gauge. Shiny groove bottom = bottoming out.
  • Replace all belts in a set together, with matched lengths.
  • Never roll belts on with a screwdriver. Move the motor in.

Timing (synchronous) belts

  • Profiles: HTD (5M, 8M, 14M), GT, T5, T10, XL, L, H.
  • Need correct tension but less than V-belts. Too loose: ratcheting (jumping teeth).
  • Pulleys must have flanges on at least one pulley.
  • Never crimp or bend tight: the tensile cords break.

Roller chains

  • ISO / ANSI sizes: pitch in eighths of an inch for ANSI (40 = 1/2", 50 = 5/8", 60 = 3/4", 80 = 1"). ISO 08B = 1/2", 10B = 5/8", 12B = 3/4", 16B = 1" (British standard dimensions).
  • Sag: about 2–3% of centre distance on horizontal drives.
  • Elongation: measure over a number of pitches under light tension. Replace at 1.5% elongation for general drives (2% for slow drives; 1% for high speed or timing).
  • Replace sprockets when teeth are hooked or pointed. Fit a new chain with new sprockets on worn drives.
  • Lubricate: drip, bath or brush with a good chain oil. Dry chains wear 10× faster.
  • Connecting link clip: closed end facing the direction of travel.
