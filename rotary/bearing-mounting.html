Bearing mounting & removal

Heating, pressing, hydraulic methods, tapered bore drive-up, adapter sleeves and safe removal.

On this page
  1. General rules
  2. Cold mounting
  3. Hot mounting
  4. Tapered bore bearings
  5. Adapter and withdrawal sleeves
  6. Removal
  7. Plummer blocks (SNL, SD, SAF)

General rules

  • Work clean. Dirt kills bearings.
  • Check shaft and housing dimensions before fitting.
  • Apply force only to the ring being fitted. Never through the rolling elements.
  • Never strike a bearing directly with a hammer.
  • Don't heat above 120 °C (most bearings). Sealed and greased bearings: 80 °C max unless the maker says more.
  • Never use an open flame.

Cold mounting

Small bearings (bore up to about 50 mm) with light interference can be pressed or driven on cold. Use a fitting tool kit: an impact ring that spreads the force on the inner ring face and a sleeve. Use a press for larger sizes.

Hot mounting

  • Induction heater: fast, clean, auto demagnetises. Use the temperature probe on the inner ring.
  • Heat to about 80–90 °C above shaft temperature. For a 100 mm bore with 0.05 mm interference, ~ 50 °C rise is enough, but 110 °C gives sliding time.
  • Slide on in one go, hold against the shoulder until it grips (it shrinks axially too).
  • Hot plate or oven: OK for small batches. Oil baths are no longer recommended (fire, contamination).
Expansion (mm) ≈ 0.000012 × bore (mm) × ΔT (°C)

Tapered bore bearings

Spherical roller and some cylindrical roller bearings have a 1:12 taper. You set the fit by driving the bearing up the taper. As it goes up, the inner ring expands and the internal clearance reduces.

Methods to judge drive-up:

  1. Clearance reduction: measure radial clearance with feelers over the unloaded rollers before and during drive-up. Stop at the reduction in the maker's table.
  2. Axial drive-up: measure axial distance travelled from the zero (start) position.
  3. Lock nut tightening angle: turn the nut a set angle from snug (small bearings).
  4. SKF Drive-up Method: hydraulic nut with a gauge to a start pressure, then a dial indicator for axial travel.

After mounting check the remaining clearance is above the minimum in the table. Fit the lock washer and bend a tab into a nut slot (tighten, never loosen, to line up).

Adapter and withdrawal sleeves

  • Adapter sleeve (H series): on plain shafts in plummer blocks. The nut pulls the bearing up the sleeve.
  • Withdrawal sleeve (AH series): pushed in under the bearing against a shaft shoulder. Removal nut pulls it out.
  • Make sure sleeve thread is clean and oiled. Use a hook spanner, hydraulic nut for big sizes.

Removal

  • Pullers: grip the ring being removed. Use a back-plate puller behind the inner ring rather than pulling on the outer ring.
  • Induction heater / aluminium heating ring for cylindrical roller inner rings with heavy fits.
  • Oil injection: pump oil into a groove in the shaft seat to float the bearing off. Keep a nut on the shaft end so it can't fly off.
  • Hydraulic nut: for withdrawal sleeves and big tapers.
  • Cutting a bearing off with a torch is a last resort. It damages the shaft and creates hazards.
Stored energy. A bearing coming off a taper or under oil pressure can release suddenly. Always fit a stop nut or plate.

Plummer blocks (SNL, SD, SAF)

  • Locate one bearing (with locating rings) and leave the other free to slide for thermal expansion.
  • Fill the housing with grease 25–40% of free space for normal speeds.
  • Base surface must be flat; shim under the full base.
  • Tighten cap bolts to the maker's torque. Caps and bases are matched pairs; don't swap.
