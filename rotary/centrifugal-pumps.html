Centrifugal pump basics

How a centrifugal pump works, its main parts, NPSH, cavitation and the checks that matter on site.

On this page

  2. Impeller types

  4. Recirculation

  7. Priming

A centrifugal pump adds energy to a liquid by spinning it. The impeller throws liquid outward, raising its velocity. The volute or diffuser then slows the liquid and turns that velocity into pressure. A pump makes head (metres of liquid), not pressure. The same pump lifts water and petrol to the same height, but the gauge reads differently because of the specific gravity.

Pressure (bar) = Head (m) × SG ÷ 10.2

Impeller types

  • Closed: shrouds on both sides of the vanes. High efficiency, clean liquids. Uses wear rings.
  • Semi-open: one shroud. Handles some solids. Efficiency depends on front clearance, set by axial adjustment (often 0.3–0.5 mm).
  • Open: no shrouds. Slurries and stringy material.
  • Single suction vs double suction: double suction halves NPSHr and balances axial thrust. Common in between-bearing pumps.

NPSH and cavitation

NPSH (net positive suction head) is how far the suction pressure sits above the liquid's vapour pressure, in metres of liquid. NPSHa is what the system gives. NPSHr is what the pump needs, from the maker's curve (the point where head falls by 3%).

NPSHa = (P_surface − P_vapour) × 10.2 ÷ SG + Z_static − H_friction

P in bar absolute, Z positive if the liquid level is above the pump centreline. Keep NPSHa above NPSHr with a margin. A common rule is NPSHa ≥ NPSHr + 1 m, or a ratio of 1.1 to 1.3. High-energy pumps, hot water and hydrocarbons near boiling need more.

Cavitation happens when pressure in the impeller eye drops below vapour pressure. Bubbles form, travel to higher pressure and collapse with great force. That collapse eats metal.

  • Sounds like gravel or marbles in the casing.
  • Head and flow fluctuate, motor amps swing.
  • Broadband vibration and noise.
  • Pitting on the low-pressure (front) side of the vanes, near the eye.

Fixes: raise suction tank level, clean the suction strainer, open the suction valve fully (never throttle suction), shorten or enlarge the suction line, cool the liquid, reduce flow toward BEP, or fit an inducer.

Recirculation

Running far left of BEP (low flow) causes suction and discharge recirculation. Liquid turns back inside the impeller. It sounds like cavitation and damages the pressure side of the vanes and the shrouds. The fix is more flow: a minimum flow bypass, not a smaller suction valve.

Operating window

  • Best: 80–110% of BEP flow.
  • Allowable: roughly 70–120% of BEP for most process pumps (API 610 preferred range).
  • Below minimum continuous flow: heating, recirculation, high vibration, seal and bearing damage.
  • Far right of the curve: high NPSHr, cavitation, motor overload.

Axial and radial thrust

Pressure acts unequally on the two sides of an impeller, making axial thrust toward suction. Makers balance it with balance holes and back wear rings, back vanes (pump-out vanes), opposed impellers in multistage pumps, or a balance drum or disc. Worn rings or blocked balance holes raise thrust and overload the thrust bearing.

Radial thrust is lowest at BEP in a single volute and rises at low and high flow. Double volutes reduce it. High radial load bends the shaft and wears the seal faces and the rings.

Priming

Most centrifugal pumps can't pump air. Fill the casing and suction line before start. Vent at the casing high point until liquid flows without bubbles. Self-priming designs keep liquid in the casing to re-prime.

Start-up checklist

  1. Permit closed, LOTO removed, guard fitted.
  2. Oil level correct, oiler filled, cooling water on.
  3. Seal flush and quench lines open, seal pot pressurised if fitted.
  4. Suction valve fully open, casing vented and primed.
  5. Discharge valve closed or cracked (centrifugal), per site procedure.
  6. Check rotation by bump if the motor was disconnected.
  7. Start, then open discharge slowly within 30 s to avoid running at shut-off.
  8. Check discharge pressure, amps, noise, seal leakage and bearing temperature.
  9. Take baseline vibration after 30 minutes.
Never run dry or dead-headed. At shut-off all motor power turns into heat in a small volume of liquid. A small pump can boil its contents in minutes.
