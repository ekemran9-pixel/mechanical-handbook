Dial indicator alignment methods

Rim-and-face and reverse-dial methods by hand, bracket sag, sign conventions and worked examples.

On this page
  1. Rules for dial readings
  2. Bracket sag
  3. Rim and face method
  4. Reverse dial method
  5. Worked example (vertical, reverse dial)
  6. Horizontal moves

Lasers are faster, but every foreman should be able to align with dial indicators. You need two dials, a solid bracket, a calculator, and patience.

Rules for dial readings

  • Zero the dial at 12 o'clock. Read at 3, 6 and 9.
  • Valid readings follow the rule: 12 + 6 = 3 + 9 (within 0.02 mm). If not, the bracket is loose, the dial sticks, or there's axial float.
  • Rim readings are TIR. Actual centreline offset = reading ÷ 2.
  • Plunger in (positive) means the surface is coming toward the dial.
  • Turn both shafts together so the dial stays on the same spot.

Bracket sag

The bracket and dial bend under gravity. At 6 o'clock the dial reads more negative than it should.

  1. Clamp the bracket on a rigid pipe of similar diameter, with the same span.
  2. Zero the dial at 12 o'clock.
  3. Rotate to 6 o'clock. Read. This is the sag (always negative, for example −0.08).
  4. Correct the bottom rim reading: subtract the sag (−0.20 measured − (−0.08) = −0.12 true).

Rim and face method

A rim dial measures offset. A face dial measures angle. Both mounted from the stationary shaft onto the movable hub.

  • Vertical offset at the dial plane = (bottom rim reading, sag corrected) ÷ 2.
  • Vertical angle = bottom face reading ÷ diameter of the face sweep (in mm/mm).
  • Front foot correction = offset + angle × (distance from dial plane to front foot).
  • Back foot correction = offset + angle × (distance to back foot).

Good for large couplings and where one shaft can't be turned (rotate only the stationary one with the dial). Face readings are spoiled by axial float. Push the shafts the same way each time.

Reverse dial method

Two rim dials. One on the stationary shaft reading the movable hub (S→M), one on the movable shaft reading the stationary hub (M→S). The distance between dial planes is the "span".

Slope = (M_rim/2 + S_rim/2) ÷ span

Then project to each foot. Sign conventions differ between textbooks, so the easiest way by hand is graphical plotting:

  1. Draw the machine to scale on graph paper: dial planes, feet, distances.
  2. Draw the stationary centreline as a horizontal line.
  3. Plot half of each corrected bottom reading at its dial plane, above or below the line by sign.
  4. Draw a straight line through the two points: that's the movable centreline.
  5. Read the distance from it to the stationary line at each foot. That's the shim change.

Reverse dial is not affected by axial float and is more accurate with long spans and spacer couplings.

Worked example (vertical, reverse dial)

Convention used here: dials zeroed at 12 o'clock, plunger pushed in reads positive, bottom readings already corrected for sag. With that convention, a positive bottom reading means the shaft being measured sits low compared with the shaft carrying the dial, by half the reading.

  • Dial A is on the stationary shaft, reading the movable (motor) hub. Bottom reading +0.40 mm. So the motor centreline is 0.20 mm low at plane A.
  • Dial B is on the motor shaft, reading the stationary hub, 200 mm away toward the pump. Bottom reading +0.10 mm. The pump is 0.05 mm low compared with the motor, so the motor is 0.05 mm high at plane B.
  • Slope of the motor line = (−0.20 − (+0.05)) ÷ 200 = −0.00125 mm per mm. It falls by 0.00125 mm for every mm you move toward the motor.
  • Front foot, 250 mm from plane A: −0.20 − 0.00125 × 250 = −0.51 mm. Add 0.51 mm of shim.
  • Back foot, 650 mm from plane A: −0.20 − 0.00125 × 650 = −1.01 mm. Add 1.01 mm of shim.

Then take a fresh set of readings. If the numbers got worse, a sign was wrong somewhere, so check your convention against the dial before moving again.

Laser check. Most sites accept laser systems (Prüftechnik, Easy-Laser, Fixturlaser, SKF) for routine work. Learn the dial methods so you can check a laser result and work when the laser is away for calibration.

Horizontal moves

Set two dials on the motor feet (front and back) on the side, touching from a fixed point. Zero them. Move with jack bolts while watching the dials until they show the calculated move. Tighten bolts and recheck: the motor often moves while tightening.
