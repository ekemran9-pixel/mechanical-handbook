Plain & tilting-pad bearings

Sleeve, journal and tilting-pad bearings, babbitt, clearances, crush and thrust bearings on big machines.

On this page

  7. Inspecting babbitt

Plain bearings carry the shaft on an oil film. With the right speed and oil, the shaft never touches the metal. They are used on large motors, turbines, compressors, big pumps and gearboxes.

Types

  • Plain sleeve (cylindrical): simple, prone to oil whirl at high speed.
  • Lemon bore / elliptical: more stable at speed.
  • Multi-lobe / pressure dam: better stability.
  • Tilting pad journal: pads pivot to form their own wedge. Most stable. Standard on high-speed turbomachinery.
  • Tilting pad thrust (Kingsbury / Michell): pads on pivots or a levelling plate, carry axial load.

Babbitt (white metal)

Tin-based babbitt (ASTM B23 grade 2 is common) on a steel shell. Soft enough to embed dirt and to wipe without scoring the shaft. Max operating metal temperature about 120–130 °C. Trip alarm on bearing RTDs is often around 115–120 °C.

Clearance

Diametral clearance is typically 0.1–0.15% of journal diameter (1.0–1.5 mm per metre). High-speed machines often use 0.15–0.2%.

Measuring clearance

  • Micrometer and bore gauge: measure journal OD and bearing ID (shells bolted together at correct torque). Most accurate.
  • Plastigage or lead wire: lay across the journal top, fit and torque the cap, remove and measure the squashed width or thickness.
  • Lift check: dial on top of the shaft, lift with a bar or jack until it stops. Reading = top clearance. Quick check without opening.
  • Feelers at the side of the bearing on a horizontal split (side clearance for elliptical bearings).

Crush (pinch)

The bearing shell must be clamped in the housing so it can't move. Crush is how much the shell stands proud of the split line before the cap is tightened. Measure with lead wire or plastigage between shell back and cap. Typical crush 0.025–0.05 mm interference. Too little and the shell spins or frets. Too much and it distorts the bore.

Thrust bearing float

  1. Mount a dial on the shaft end.
  2. Push the rotor fully one way with a bar (not on a seal or labyrinth).
  3. Zero the dial, push fully the other way.
  4. Reading = total float. Typical 0.25–0.40 mm on compressors and turbines, follow OEM.
  5. Adjust with shims behind the thrust pad carrier.

Oil supply

  • Pressure lube systems: header pressure usually 1.0–1.7 bar g at the bearings.
  • Oil supply temperature 40–50 °C, drain temperature rise about 10–25 °C.
  • Check orifices in each bearing supply line are the correct size and clean.
  • Ring-oiled bearings: check the oil ring turns and isn't distorted; level per mark.
