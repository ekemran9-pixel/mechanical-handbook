Centrifugal compressors

Surge, anti-surge control, dry gas seals, lube and seal oil systems, rotor checks and overhaul measurements.

On this page

  2. Dry gas seals
  3. Lube oil console
  4. Rotor checks at overhaul

Surge

At low flow the compressor can't keep up its pressure ratio. Flow reverses through the machine, then recovers, many times a second. You hear and feel heavy banging, and see pressure and flow swinging. Surge damages thrust bearings, seals, labyrinths and impellers.

  • The surge line on the map marks the minimum stable flow at each speed.
  • The anti-surge controller opens a recycle valve to keep flow a margin (often 10%) to the right of the surge line.
  • Never block or manually close the anti-surge valve while running.
  • Stonewall (choke): at very high flow the gas reaches sonic speed in the impeller and head collapses.

Dry gas seals

Two rings: a rotating ring with spiral grooves and a stationary ring. The grooves pump gas between the faces and lift them apart by about 3–5 µm. No contact when running.

  • Primary seal gas: clean, dry, filtered process gas or N₂, supplied above process pressure.
  • Separation gas (N₂ or air) at the barrier seal keeps bearing oil out of the gas seal.
  • Separation gas must be on before the lube oil starts and stay on after it stops. Oil reaching the seal faces destroys them.
  • Watch primary vent flow trends. Rising vent flow = primary seal wear.
  • Seal gas must be above dew point (heaters) and filtered to 1–3 µm. Liquids and dirt are the main killers.
  • Never rotate the rotor backwards. Unidirectional seals fail.

Lube oil console

  • Main and auxiliary oil pumps. Auxiliary starts on low pressure.
  • Twin coolers and twin filters with changeover valve. Fill and vent the standby side before switching.
  • Rundown tank or accumulator to supply oil during coast-down after a power failure.
  • Oil temperature to bearings 40–50 °C.
  • Test auxiliary pump auto-start regularly.

Rotor checks at overhaul

  • Total rotor float (thrust bearings removed): records the axial position of impellers relative to diffusers.
  • Thrust bearing float and position. Set the rotor so impellers line up with diffuser channels.
  • Journal bearing clearances, pad thickness.
  • Labyrinth clearances (diametral), by feeler or lead.
  • Rotor runout at journals, probe areas and impeller eyes.
  • Probe area runout (mechanical + electrical) for proximity probes: typical limit 25% of allowable vibration or 6 µm.
  • Impeller inspection: fouling, erosion, cracks (dye penetrant or MT on blades and eye).
  • Balance piston and its labyrinth: worn balance labyrinths overload the thrust bearing.

Slow roll and barring

After shutdown, a hot rotor bends if left still (top cools faster). Keep it turning on the turning gear until the casing cools. Before start-up, slow roll to check runout at the probes is within normal (shows bow).
