Mechanical seals

Seal designs, materials, installation, setting length and failure reading for pump mechanical seals.

On this page
  1. Parts
  2. Designs
  3. Arrangements (API 682)
  4. Face materials
  5. Elastomers
  6. Installing a cartridge seal
  7. Component seals
  8. Reading a failed seal

A mechanical seal has two flat faces, one rotating with the shaft and one fixed in the gland. Springs or a bellows hold them together. A liquid film about 1 µm thick separates them. Faces are lapped flat to within 2–3 light bands (about 0.6–0.9 µm).

Parts

  • Primary ring (usually the flexibly mounted one, often carbon).
  • Mating ring (seat, usually hard: SiC or tungsten carbide).
  • Secondary seals: O-rings, wedges or bellows that seal the faces to shaft and gland.
  • Springs: single coil spring, multiple small springs, or a metal bellows.
  • Drive: set screws, drive pins or lugs.
  • Gland plate with flush, quench, drain and vent ports.
  • Sleeve (cartridge seals) with a locking collar.

Designs

DesignFeatureUse
PusherO-ring slides along the sleeve as faces wearGeneral service up to ~175 °C. O-ring can hang up on deposits
Non-pusher (metal bellows)Bellows flexes, no sliding sealHot service up to ~400 °C with graphite seals, dirty or crystallising fluids
Elastomer bellowsRubber bellowsWater pumps, low cost
BalancedFace area adjusted so closing force is lowerPressures above ~10 bar
UnbalancedFull pressure on the faceLow pressure, simple pumps
Rotating springSprings turn with the shaftMost common, simple
Stationary springSprings in the glandHigh speed, better tolerance to misalignment

Arrangements (API 682)

  • Arrangement 1: one seal per cartridge.
  • Arrangement 2: two seals, buffer fluid between at lower pressure than the seal chamber (unpressurised dual).
  • Arrangement 3: two seals, barrier fluid at higher pressure than the seal chamber (pressurised dual). Product cannot leak out.

Face materials

PairUse
Carbon vs SiCThe best general pair. Low friction, good heat removal
Carbon vs tungsten carbideGood, slightly more heat
SiC vs SiCAbrasives, slurries. Poor dry-running tolerance
TC vs TCSlurries, high pressure
Carbon vs ceramic (alumina)Water pumps, low cost. Ceramic cracks under thermal shock

Installing a cartridge seal

  1. Check shaft runout (≤ 0.05 mm), end float (≤ 0.10 mm) and radial play.
  2. Check seal chamber face runout and bore concentricity.
  3. Remove burrs and sharp edges on the shaft, keyways and threads. Tape them over.
  4. Lubricate the shaft and sleeve O-ring lightly with compatible lube.
  5. Slide on without twisting. Don't push on the faces.
  6. Bolt the gland evenly to the chamber with its gasket.
  7. Set impeller position first (axial adjust) on pumps that need it, then tighten the sleeve collar set screws.
  8. Remove the setting clips. Store them on the pump or in the job card.
  9. Connect flush, quench and vent. Vent the chamber fully before start.

Component seals

For a component seal, scribe a reference mark on the shaft at the seal chamber face, then measure the setting length from the drawing to set the rotating unit. Wrong setting length gives too much or too little spring load.

Reading a failed seal

What you seeLikely cause
Heat check cracks on hard faceDry running, poor flush, vapourisation
Chipped carbon edgesFaces opening and slamming (flashing, vibration)
Wide wear trackShaft runout, misalignment, bent shaft
Narrow track off centreMating ring not square, gland not concentric
Eccentric trackGland not centred on the shaft
No wear on part of the faceFace distortion: bolts uneven, gasket thick
Carbon blisteringViscous fluid, thermal cycling
Grooved faceAbrasive particles
Swollen O-ringChemical attack, wrong compound
Hard, cracked O-ringOver temperature
Extruded O-ringHigh pressure, large gap
Hang-up, deposits behind facesCrystallising fluid, no quench
Broken springsCorrosion or fatigue
Fretted sleeve under O-ringVibration, pusher movement
Leakage limit. A healthy single seal leaks a vapour or a few drops per hour you can't usually see. Steady drips mean the seal has failed. Dual seals show failure as a change in buffer or barrier pot level or pressure.
