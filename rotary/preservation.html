Storage & preservation

Preserving idle and spare rotating equipment: rotation schedules, VCI, nitrogen blanketing and long-term storage.

On this page
  1. Standby equipment in service
  2. Long-term storage (warehouse)
  3. Mothballing a unit
  4. Recommissioning

Standby equipment in service

  • Run standby pumps on a schedule (e.g. weekly or monthly changeover) or at least rotate the shaft by hand a few turns each week to a different position (e.g. 1¼ turns).
  • Keep oil in housings at the correct level. Check for water.
  • Keep seal support systems live on standby pumps.

Long-term storage (warehouse)

  • Indoors, dry, heated, low vibration. Away from forklifts.
  • Fill bearing housings with preservative oil or fill completely with oil (drain to normal level before start).
  • Spray bare machined surfaces with rust preventive. Wrap with VCI paper.
  • Cap all openings. Desiccant bags inside with a humidity indicator.
  • Rotate shafts monthly. Record it on a tag.
  • Large rotors: store vertically or on a cradle supported at the journals, rotated 180° regularly to avoid sag.
  • Elastomers (O-rings, seals): cool, dark, in sealed bags. Shelf life by material (e.g. NBR ~5–7 years, FKM ~15–20 years per ISO 2230).

Mothballing a unit

  • Nitrogen blanket at slight positive pressure for compressors and turbines.
  • Dehumidified air circulation for large systems.
  • Isolate and drain cooling water systems, or treat with inhibitor.
  • Inspection and rotation log with dates and signatures.

Recommissioning

  1. Inspect for corrosion, water, nests and debris.
  2. Drain preservative oil, flush, fill with service oil.
  3. Replace desiccant breathers and elastomers if aged.
  4. Check alignment, megger motors.
  5. Run-in and take baselines.
