Seal flush plans (API 682)

Every common API 682 piping plan, what it does, how to operate it and what goes wrong.

On this page
  1. Single seal plans (process side)
  2. Dual seal plans (between the seals)
  3. Atmospheric side plans
  4. Gas seal plans (containment seals)
  5. Plan 53A filling and start-up

The flush plan controls the environment around the seal faces: temperature, cleanliness, pressure and lubrication. Each plan has a number. The drawing on the pump data sheet shows which plan you have.

02Dead-ended chamber, no flushCool, clean, low pressure. Often with cooling jacketVapour builds up

14Plan 11 + Plan 13Vertical pumps needing flush and venting
21Plan 11 through a coolerHot fluidsCooler fouling, high cooling load
23Closed loop through cooler using a pumping ringHot water, boiler feed. Most efficient coolingMust vent the loop high point on start
31Plan 11 through a cyclone separatorSolids heavier than the fluidNeeds ΔP across the cyclone
32External clean flush injectedDirty, abrasive, polymerising fluidsDilutes product, flush must be above chamber pressure

PlanArrangementPurposeOperate

PlanPurpose

Gas seal plans (containment seals)

  • Plan 72: buffer gas (N₂) into the containment seal cavity.
  • Plan 74: pressurised gas barrier for dual gas seals.
  • Plan 75: leakage collection for condensing leakage.
  • Plan 76: leakage vented to flare for non-condensing leakage.

Plan 53A filling and start-up

  1. Fill the pot with clean barrier fluid (often a light oil or water/glycol) to the normal level.
  2. Vent the barrier circuit at the seal high point until fluid comes out free of air.
  3. Pressurise with N₂ to the set value above seal chamber pressure.
  4. Check the pumping ring is circulating: the return line to the pot warms after start.
  5. Record level and pressure each shift. A trend tells you which seal is leaking.
Diagnosing with the pot. Plan 52: level up = process leaking in through the inner seal. Plan 53: level down with no visible leak = barrier leaking into the process through the inner seal.
