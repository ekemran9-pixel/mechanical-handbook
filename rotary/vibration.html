Vibration analysis

Units, measurement points, ISO 10816/20816 limits, the main fault signatures, phase analysis and route planning.

Units

QuantityUnitBest for
Displacementµm pk-pk (mils)Low speed, shaft movement in sleeve bearings (proximity probes)
Velocitymm/s RMS (in/s pk)Overall machine condition 10–1000 Hz. The standard for most plant machines
Accelerationg (m/s²)High frequency: bearing defects, gear mesh

Conversions: in/s pk × 25.4 ÷ 1.414 = mm/s RMS. 0.3 in/s pk ≈ 5.4 mm/s RMS.

Measurement points

  • On every bearing housing: horizontal (H), vertical (V) and axial (A).
  • As close to the bearing load zone as possible, on solid metal, not on covers or fins.
  • Same spot every time. Mark it or fit a stud.
  • Magnet mounting is good to about 2 kHz (flat rare-earth magnet higher). Hand-held probes only for quick checks.
  • Machine at normal load and temperature.

ISO 10816-3 / 20816-3 zones

MachineFoundationA/BB/CC/D
Group 2: 15–300 kWRigid1.42.84.5

Group 1: 300 kW–50 MWRigid2.34.57.1
Group 1Flexible3.57.111.0

mm/s RMS, 10–1000 Hz on bearing housings. A = new. B = unrestricted long-term. C = limited period, plan repair. D = damage likely. A foundation is "rigid" if the machine's lowest natural frequency on it is at least 25% above running speed.

API 610 pumps: overall limit for new pumps on test is typically 3.0 mm/s RMS (bearing housing), within the preferred operating range.

Trending is more useful than limits

A machine running steadily at 4 mm/s may be fine. One that went from 1.5 to 3 mm/s in a month has a problem developing. A doubling from baseline is a common alert level.

Spectrum basics

The FFT spectrum splits the signal into frequencies. Everything is related to running speed: 1X = running speed. A 2960 rpm motor: 1X = 49.3 Hz.

Bearing defect frequencies

  • BPFO (ball pass outer race): about 0.4 × number of balls × rpm.
  • BPFI (inner race): about 0.6 × number of balls × rpm, with 1X sidebands.
  • BSF (ball spin), FTF (cage, about 0.4X).
  • Software calculates them from the bearing number. Inner race defects show sidebands at 1X because the defect rotates in and out of the load zone.

Stages of bearing failure: 1) ultrasonic only (stress waves, > 20 kHz), 2) envelope/demod peaks at defect frequencies, 3) defect frequencies visible in the velocity spectrum with harmonics, 4) noise floor rises, 1X rises, discrete peaks disappear into haystack. Replace in stage 3 at the latest.

Phase analysis

Phase compares the timing of vibration at two points, using a tachometer reference or two channels. It tells unbalance from misalignment from bent shaft when the spectra look the same. Take phase in H, V and A at each bearing.

Bump test

With the machine stopped, hit the structure with a soft-faced mallet and capture the ring-down spectrum. Peaks show natural frequencies. A natural frequency within ±20% of 1X or another forcing frequency is a resonance risk.

Quick power-off test

Watch the live reading and cut the power. If the vibration drops instantly, the cause is electrical. If it coasts down with speed, it's mechanical.
