Shaft alignment

Why alignment matters, the full alignment procedure, soft foot, pipe strain, tolerances and thermal growth.

Misalignment loads bearings, seals and couplings. It shows up as high axial vibration, warm couplings, short bearing and seal life and wasted energy. Good alignment is the cheapest reliability improvement you can make.

Terms

  • Offset (parallel): the distance between shaft centrelines, measured at the coupling centre. Expressed in mm.
  • Angularity: the angle between centrelines. Expressed as a gap difference per 100 mm of diameter (mm/100 mm), or in mrad (1 mrad = 0.1 mm/100 mm).
  • Both are measured in the vertical plane and the horizontal plane: four values in total.
  • Stationary machine: the one you don't move (usually the pump or gearbox). Movable machine: usually the motor.

Procedure

  1. Safety: LOTO the driver, isolate the driven machine. Check both shafts can be turned.
  2. Prepare: clean the baseplate, feet and shims. Check bolts and holes. Have a set of precut stainless shims.
  3. Check run-out of the coupling hubs (≤ 0.05 mm) and that the shafts aren't bent.
  4. Rough align with a straight edge and feelers to within about 0.5 mm.
  5. Check soft foot on every foot and correct it (below).
  6. Check pipe strain on the stationary machine.
  7. Measure with laser or dials. Record as-found values.
  8. Correct vertical: add or remove shims under the movable machine's front and back feet.
  9. Correct horizontal: move sideways with jack bolts, with dials on the feet to watch the move.
  10. Tighten the hold-down bolts in a set sequence and to torque.
  11. Re-measure. Repeat until in tolerance.
  12. Record final readings, shims added, coupling gap. Refit guard.

Soft foot

Soft foot means one foot doesn't sit flat. Tightening its bolt twists the frame, distorts bearing bores and changes alignment.

  1. Tighten all foot bolts.
  2. Put a dial on one foot (or use the laser soft foot program).
  3. Loosen that bolt only. Read the rise.
  4. Retighten. Repeat for every foot.
  5. Any foot rising more than 0.05 mm needs correction.

Pipe strain check

Mount dials on the stationary shaft or coupling in vertical and horizontal planes. Loosen the suction and discharge flange bolts. If the shaft moves more than 0.05 mm, the piping is forcing the machine. Fix supports or re-fit the spool. Never bend a pump to suit the pipe.

Spacer couplings are judged by angle across each flex element: a common limit is 0.5 mm per metre of spacer length (0.5 mrad) for 3000 rpm.

Correcting with shims

With rim-face or laser data you calculate corrections at the front feet and back feet. The laser does it for you. By hand:

Foot correction = Offset + (Angle × Distance from coupling centre to foot)

Use angle as a slope (mm per mm). Watch the sign: positive means the movable machine is high and you remove shims.

Bolt bound and base bound

  • Bolt bound: the motor can't move far enough sideways because the bolts hit the holes. Move the stationary machine slightly, or open the holes on the movable one (with engineering approval).
  • Base bound: the motor needs to go lower but there are no shims left. Raise the stationary machine or machine the base.

Thermal growth

Machines grow as they warm. A hot pump centreline rises; a motor grows a little. Set the cold alignment so the shafts come into line hot.

ΔL (mm) = α × L (mm) × ΔT (°C) steel α = 0.0000117

Example: pump centreline 450 mm above the base, casing support temperature rises 60 °C: ΔL = 0.0000117 × 450 × 60 = 0.32 mm. Set the pump 0.32 mm low compared with the motor cold (or the motor 0.32 mm high), minus the motor's own growth.

Makers often give cold offsets on the alignment drawing. Hot alignment checks (with probes, or Dodd bars, or a laser hot check right after shutdown) confirm them.

Vertical machines

Vertical pumps with a rigid motor stand are checked for squareness of the motor mounting face and concentricity (rabbet fit). Shim between motor and stand only if the design allows.

Good practice

  • Turn both shafts together in the direction of rotation for readings.
  • Don't use a coupling spider as a lever for turning.
  • Use precut stainless shims, never more than 4 per foot. Mark the thickness.
  • Leave the movable machine with some shim under every foot (0.5–1 mm) for future correction.
  • Recheck alignment after the first 24–48 h of running on new installations.
