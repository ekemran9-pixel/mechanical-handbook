Screw & air compressors

Oil-flooded and oil-free screw compressors, air system controls, routine service and common faults.

On this page
  1. Oil-flooded screw
  2. Oil-free screw
  3. Control modes
  4. Routine service (typical)
  5. Common faults
  6. Air receivers

Oil-flooded screw

  • Oil injected into the rotors seals, cools and lubricates. Discharge temperature 70–100 °C.
  • Separator element strips oil from the air. Replace when ΔP reaches the limit (often 0.7–1 bar) or by hours.
  • Minimum pressure valve keeps pressure in the separator for oil circulation.
  • Thermostatic valve sends oil through or around the cooler to keep discharge above the dew point.
  • Scavenge line returns oil from the separator. A blocked orifice gives high oil carry-over.

Oil-free screw

  • Rotors don't touch. Timing gears keep them in phase. Two stages with an intercooler.
  • Rotor coatings wear: performance falls. Element exchange at OEM hours.
  • Check intercooler and aftercooler, drains and condensate traps.

Control modes

  • Load/unload: inlet valve opens or closes between pressure set points. Unloaded power is still 25–35% of full load.
  • Modulation: throttles the inlet. Inefficient at low flow.
  • VSD: varies speed. Best efficiency for varying demand.
  • Use one VSD trim machine with fixed-speed base load machines. Use a master controller to sequence them.

Routine service (typical)

IntervalTask
DailyCheck oil level, discharge temp, pressure, alarms. Drain receiver and traps
WeeklyCheck air filter indicator, cooler cleanliness, condensate drains
2,000 hAir filter, oil filter
4,000–8,000 hOil change (depends on oil type), separator element
YearlySafety valve test, minimum pressure valve, thermostatic valve, hoses
24,000–40,000 hBearings / element overhaul

Air receivers

  • Pressure vessels: need inspection per local law, safety valve set at or below design pressure.
  • Drain daily. Fit zero-loss drains instead of timer drains.
  • Check for corrosion at the drain area.
