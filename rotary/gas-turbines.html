Gas turbines (overview)

Gas turbine sections, borescope inspection, typical maintenance intervals and what the site mechanical team does.

On this page
  1. Sections
  2. Maintenance intervals
  3. Routine site tasks

Gas turbines are usually maintained under an OEM long-term service agreement, but the site mechanical team supports every outage and does the routine work.

Sections

  • Inlet: filter house (pulse-jet or static), silencer, anti-icing.
  • Compressor: axial, many stages. Inlet guide vanes (IGVs) control airflow.
  • Combustion: can-annular or annular combustors, fuel nozzles, igniters, flame detectors.
  • Turbine: hot gas path (HGP) stages, nozzles and buckets with cooling holes.
  • Exhaust: diffuser, to stack or heat recovery steam generator (HRSG).

Maintenance intervals

Based on equivalent operating hours (EOH) or starts (whichever comes first), because starts and trips cause thermal fatigue.

  • Combustion inspection: every 8,000–12,000 EOH (varies a lot by model).
  • Hot gas path inspection: every 24,000 EOH.
  • Major overhaul: every 48,000 EOH.

Routine site tasks

  • Inlet filter ΔP monitoring and change-out.
  • Compressor washing: online (running) and offline (crank wash). Restores power lost to fouling, often 2–5%.
  • Borescope inspections of compressor, combustors and HGP between outages.
  • Lube oil system, filters, coolers, vent fans.
  • Fuel gas filters and coalescers.
  • Enclosure ventilation, fire and gas detection, CO₂ or water-mist systems (with lockout before entry).
Enclosure entry. Gas turbine enclosures have CO₂ fire suppression. Lock out the system and follow the entry permit before going in.
