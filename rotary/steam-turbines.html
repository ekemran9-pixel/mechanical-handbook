Steam turbines

Types, warm-up, overspeed protection, governing, gland sealing, trips and overhaul checks.

On this page
  1. Types
  2. Protection
  3. Overspeed trip test
  4. Warm-up and start
  5. Governor
  6. Overhaul checks

Types

  • Back pressure: exhausts to a lower pressure steam header. Common for pump and fan drives.
  • Condensing: exhausts to a vacuum condenser. Most power from the steam.
  • Extraction: steam taken out at an intermediate stage for process.
  • Impulse (steam expands in nozzles, pushes blades) vs reaction (expands in both fixed and moving blades). Most small turbines are impulse (Curtis or Rateau stages).

Protection

  • Overspeed trip: mechanical bolt or electronic. Typical trip at 110% of maximum continuous speed (MCS). API 611/612.
  • Trip and throttle valve (T&T): closes on a trip. Must be exercised regularly to prove it moves freely.
  • Other trips: low lube oil pressure, high vibration, high axial displacement, high exhaust pressure, low vacuum.

Overspeed trip test

  1. Done with operations and a procedure. Uncoupled (solo) for the first test after overhaul if required.
  2. Confirm speed indication is accurate (calibrated tachometer).
  3. Raise speed slowly with the governor override or test device.
  4. Record the trip speed. Repeat to confirm (often 3 times within ±1%).
  5. Adjust the mechanical trip spring if out of range, per OEM.
  6. Never bypass or gag the trip to run the turbine.

Warm-up and start

  1. Lube oil on, temperature at least 25–30 °C.
  2. Open all casing and steam line drains. Warm the inlet line to the T&T valve.
  3. Start the gland seal steam (condensing) and then pull vacuum.
  4. Crack the T&T valve, roll the rotor slowly (often 300–500 rpm) and hold for the warm-up time.
  5. Listen for rubs. Watch vibration and casing expansion.
  6. Increase speed through criticals quickly to the governor range.
  7. Close drains when dry steam appears.
Water induction from undrained lines can bend rotors, crack blades and break thrust bearings. Drain properly and never rush a cold start.

Governor

Small turbines use a mechanical-hydraulic governor (e.g. Woodward TG, PG) or an electronic one. Check governor oil level and grade, linkage free play, and droop setting (often 4%). Hunting speed: linkage wear, wrong compensation needle setting, sticky governor valve.

Overhaul checks

  • Journal and thrust bearing clearances, thrust float.
  • Rotor runout, blade tip and shroud condition, cracks (dye penetrant on blade roots and disc).
  • Erosion of last-stage blades (wet steam) and nozzles.
  • Labyrinth and carbon ring gland seals: replace carbon rings, set clearances.
  • Governor valve and T&T valve: stem straightness, seat condition, packing.
  • Casing horizontal joint: clean, check flatness, use correct compound sparingly, torque bolts hot or by stretch where specified.
  • Steam strainer at the inlet: clean and check for missing pieces.
