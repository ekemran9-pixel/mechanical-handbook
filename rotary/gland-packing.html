Gland packing

Selecting, cutting, installing and adjusting compression packing on pumps and valves.

On this page
  1. Materials
  2. Sizing
  3. Cutting rings
  4. Installation
  5. Running in
  6. Valve packing

Compression packing is rings of braided material squeezed in the stuffing box by a gland. It must leak a little. The leakage cools and lubricates the packing.

Materials

PackingUse
PTFE-impregnated syntheticGeneral water, chemicals
Expanded PTFE with graphite (GFO)General pumps. Good heat transfer, low friction
Aramid (Kevlar)Abrasive slurries. Hard on sleeves
Flexible graphite die-formed ringsValves, high temperature steam
Carbon fibreHigh temperature, high speed

Sizing

Packing size = (Box bore − Shaft dia) ÷ 2

Number of rings = box depth ÷ packing size (allow for the lantern ring if fitted).

Cutting rings

  • Wrap the packing around a mandrel the same size as the shaft. Never stretch it around.
  • Cut with a sharp knife at 45° (skive cut) for pumps. Square cut is also used; follow the packing maker.
  • Check each ring fits the shaft with no gap at the joint.

Installation

  1. Remove all old packing with packing hooks. Don't scratch the sleeve.
  2. Clean the box. Check the sleeve: replace if grooved more than 0.25 mm.
  3. Check sleeve runout (≤ 0.08 mm) and box concentricity.
  4. Fit rings one at a time, seating each with a split tamping tool or the gland.
  5. Stagger the joints 90° (4 rings) or 120° (3 rings).
  6. Place the lantern ring in line with the flush port. Measure where it needs to be before you start.
  7. Fit the gland, nuts finger tight, then snug evenly. Don't crush.

Running in

  1. Start with heavy leakage (a steady stream).
  2. Let it run 10–15 minutes.
  3. Tighten the gland nuts one flat (1/6 turn) at a time, evenly.
  4. Wait 10 minutes between adjustments.
  5. Aim for about 10–20 drops per minute per 25 mm of shaft dia, or the maker's figure.
  6. Gland should be cool enough to touch. Hot gland = over tight.
Never run packing dry. No leakage means it's burning. The shaft sleeve scores and you get a big leak soon after.

Valve packing

  • Use die-formed graphite rings with braided anti-extrusion end rings for steam and hot service.
  • Torque gland bolts to the packing maker's value, evenly.
  • Live-loading (Belleville springs) keeps load on packing as it consolidates. Good for emissions (LDAR).
  • Stroke the valve several times, then re-torque.
