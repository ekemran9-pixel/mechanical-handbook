Electric motors (mechanical side)

What a mechanical foreman checks on induction motors: frames, bearings, cooling, insulation basics, VFD effects and storage.

On this page

  2. Speed
  3. Bearings
  4. Shaft currents and VFDs
  5. Cooling
  6. Insulation resistance (for information)
  7. Mechanical checks
  8. Storage of spare motors

Electrical isolation, terminal work and testing belong to the electricians. The mechanical team usually handles bearings, couplings, alignment, mounting, cooling and lubrication.

Nameplate

  • Power (kW or HP), voltage, current, frequency, speed (rpm at full load), power factor, efficiency class (IE2, IE3, IE4).
  • Frame size (IEC: shaft height in mm, e.g. 160M; NEMA: e.g. 254T).
  • Bearing numbers DE (drive end) and NDE (non-drive end), grease type and interval.
  • Insulation class (F = 155 °C, H = 180 °C), IP rating, duty type (S1 continuous).
  • Ex marking for hazardous areas: don't modify these motors without certified repair.

Speed

Synchronous speed = 120 × f ÷ poles

At 50 Hz: 2-pole 3000 rpm, 4-pole 1500, 6-pole 1000, 8-pole 750. Actual speed is lower by the slip (1–5%), e.g. 2960 rpm.

Bearings

  • Usually deep groove ball bearings, C3 clearance. Large motors: cylindrical roller at the DE (for belt loads) and ball at the NDE (locating).
  • Roller bearing DE on a direct coupled motor with little radial load can skid and fail. Check the bearing suits the drive type.
  • Grease: often polyurea or lithium complex NLGI 2–3. Don't mix. Follow the nameplate interval.
  • Locked bearing on the NDE, floating on the DE (or as the maker specifies).

Shaft currents and VFDs

Variable frequency drives create common-mode voltage that discharges through the bearings, causing fluting. Signs: frosted raceways, washboard pattern, early failures on VFD motors.

  • Fit insulated bearings (ceramic-coated outer ring) at the NDE, or hybrid ceramic balls.
  • Fit a shaft grounding ring or brush at the DE.
  • Use proper VFD cable with symmetrical earth and 360° gland earthing.

Cooling

  • TEFC motors (totally enclosed fan cooled): keep fins clean. Every 10 °C over rating halves insulation life.
  • Check the fan cowl isn't blocked or dented onto the fan.
  • Large motors: check air filters, heat exchangers (air-to-air or air-to-water), and space heaters (on when stopped to prevent condensation).

Insulation resistance (for information)

The electrician meggers the winding before starting a motor that has been idle or wet. A common minimum is 1 MΩ plus 1 MΩ per kV of rating; many sites use 100 MΩ as healthy for LV. Polarisation index (PI = 10-min/1-min reading) above 2 is good.

Mechanical checks

  • Soft foot and alignment, as for pumps.
  • Shaft runout and end float (sleeve bearing motors have a magnetic centre marked on the shaft; set the coupling so the rotor runs at magnetic centre within the float limits).
  • Feet and base bolts tight.
  • Drain holes at the bottom of the frame open (condensation).
  • Rotation: uncouple and bump-test with the electrician before coupling to direction-sensitive machines.

Storage of spare motors

  • Dry, heated store. Space heaters energised if fitted.
  • Rotate the shaft several turns every month to redistribute grease and prevent false brinelling.
  • Lock the rotor for transport on roller bearing motors (transport lock).
  • Megger before installing.
