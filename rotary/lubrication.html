Lubrication

Oil and grease selection, ISO VG grades, NLGI, relubrication amounts and intervals, compatibility and good practice.

Most bearing failures trace back to lubrication: wrong type, wrong amount, contamination or none at all. The five rights: right lubricant, right amount, right place, right time, right method.

Viscosity

Viscosity is the most important property. ISO VG number = kinematic viscosity in cSt (mm²/s) at 40 °C. VG 68 is 61.2–74.8 cSt.

ISO VGTypical application
10–22Spindles, light hydraulics, instrument air lubricators
32Steam and gas turbines, centrifugal compressors, hydraulics
46Hydraulics, turbines, some pump bearings
68Most pump and fan oil-bath bearings, air compressors
100Larger bearings, recip compressor crankcases, warm climates
150–220Enclosed gearboxes (spur, helical), slow bearings
320Heavy gearboxes, slow high load
460–680Worm gears, very slow heavy gears
1000+Open gears (often with additive or as a compound)

Rule of thumb for bearings: the oil needs to be at least about 13 cSt at operating temperature for ball bearings and 20 cSt for roller bearings. Higher speed needs thinner oil, higher load needs thicker.

Oil types

  • R&O (rust and oxidation inhibited): turbines, compressors, circulating systems, most pumps.
  • AW (antiwear): hydraulics. Contains zinc (ZDDP) or ashless antiwear.
  • EP (extreme pressure): gearboxes. Sulphur-phosphorus additives. Avoid EP in systems with bronze worm wheels unless rated, and in turbines.
  • Synthetic PAO: wide temperature range, long life, compatible with mineral oil.
  • PAG (polyglycol): worm gears, gas compressors. Not compatible with mineral oil and many paints and seals.
  • Ester: fire-resistant hydraulics, biodegradable.

Grease

Grease = base oil (70–95%) + thickener + additives. The base oil does the lubricating. The thickener holds it like a sponge.

Compatibility

Mixing incompatible greases can make the mix soften and run out, or harden. Lithium complex is generally compatible with lithium and calcium complex; polyurea is often incompatible with lithium. When changing grease type, purge the old grease out fully and shorten the next interval.

How much grease

Gp (grams) = 0.005 × D × B

D = bearing outside diameter (mm), B = width (mm). This is the replenishment quantity for side-greased bearings (SKF formula). A grease gun stroke is typically 1–1.5 g; weigh a few strokes from your gun to find out.

Initial fill: 30–50% of free space in the bearing, housing 30–50% for normal speed. Over-filling causes churning and heat.

How often

Intervals depend on speed (n × dm), temperature, type and contamination. Halve the interval for every 15 °C above 70 °C. Vertical shafts: halve. Dirty or wet conditions: shorten more. Use the maker's chart or a calculator, and confirm with ultrasound or temperature trending.

Greasing procedure

  1. Clean the nipple and the gun tip.
  2. Remove the drain plug if fitted.
  3. Pump the calculated amount slowly. With ultrasound, stop when the noise stops falling.
  4. Run the machine 20–30 minutes with the drain open to purge excess.
  5. Refit the drain plug. Record date and amount.

Oil bath levels

  • Horizontal shafts, ball bearings: oil level to the centre of the lowest ball (stopped).
  • Higher speeds: lower level, or use an oil ring or flinger.
  • Constant level oilers: mount on the side where rotation carries oil away from the oiler (against rotation), and set the height to the housing mark. Many have the arrow; follow it.
  • Check housing vents. Pressurised housings push oil out of the seals.

Oil mist

Pure mist supplies fresh oil continuously to bearings and keeps housings under slight positive pressure, keeping out dirt and moisture. Common in refineries. Check the mist generator, header pressure, and reclassifier fittings at each bearing. A blocked fitting starves the bearing silently.

Storage and handling

  • Store drums under cover, on their side with bungs at 3 and 9 o'clock.
  • Label every container and grease gun with the product name. Colour-code by lubricant.
  • Use filter carts and desiccant breathers on reservoirs.
  • Use clean, dedicated transfer containers. Never open buckets.
