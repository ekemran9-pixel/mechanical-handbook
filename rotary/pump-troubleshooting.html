Pump troubleshooting

Symptom-by-symptom fault finding for centrifugal pumps, from no flow to short seal life.

On this page
  1. No flow or very low flow
  2. Flow or head lower than design
  3. Motor overload
  4. Vibration and noise
  5. Bearings running hot
  6. Short seal life
  7. Pump loses prime after running
  8. Good questions to ask the operator

Work from the outside in. Check the system before opening the pump. Most pump problems are suction problems.

No flow or very low flow

  • Pump not primed, or air lock in the suction line (high spots).
  • Suction valve closed, strainer blocked, foot valve stuck.
  • Wrong rotation. Check by bumping the motor. 3-phase motors reverse when two phases are swapped.
  • Speed too low (VFD setpoint, belt slip).
  • Discharge head higher than shut-off (blocked line, closed downstream valve, wrong valve line-up).
  • Impeller blocked or loose on the shaft (key sheared).
  • Air leak at the suction: packing, flange gaskets, cracks. Air entrainment from a vortex at low tank level.

Flow or head lower than design

  • Worn wear rings or impeller. Compare with the curve at the same flow.
  • Gas in the liquid, near boiling liquid.
  • Higher viscosity than design.
  • Impeller trimmed or wrong impeller fitted.
  • Semi-open impeller front clearance too large.

Motor overload

  • Operating far right on the curve: low system resistance (a burst line, a bypass open).
  • Higher SG or viscosity than design.
  • Mechanical rubbing: wear rings, impeller to casing, bent shaft.
  • Packing too tight.
  • Electrical: low voltage, single phasing.

Bearings running hot

  • Too much grease, or oil level too high (churning).
  • Oil level too low, oiler empty or on wrong side.
  • Wrong oil viscosity, water in oil.
  • Misalignment, too tight a fit, C3 bearing needed but CN fitted.
  • High thrust from worn wear rings or blocked balance holes.
  • Cooling water off on cooled housings.

Short seal life

  • Dry running at start-up or from vapour in the seal chamber. Vent properly.
  • Flush orifice blocked, cooler fouled.
  • Running off BEP: shaft deflection.
  • Misalignment, pipe strain, bent shaft, worn bearings.
  • Setting clips left in, or wrong setting length on a component seal.
  • Elastomer not compatible with the product or flush.
  • Solids in the seal chamber.

Pump loses prime after running

  • Suction air leak.
  • Liquid level falls below the vortex limit.
  • Gas coming out of the liquid (dissolved gas, temperature rise).

Good questions to ask the operator

  1. When did it start? After maintenance, after a process change, slowly or suddenly?
  2. Is the flow and pressure the same as usual?
  3. Has the product changed (temperature, grade, density)?
  4. Is the spare pump doing the same?
  5. Any valves changed around the system?
