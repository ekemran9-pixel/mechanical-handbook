Torque & preload

Why preload matters, torque formula, standard torque tables for metric bolts, torque tools and calibration.

Why preload matters

A bolt works like a spring. Tightened properly, the clamped parts carry the working load and the bolt sees little change in stress. Under-tightened, the joint separates, slips and the bolt fatigues or loosens.

Torque formula

T (N·m) = K × F (kN) × d (mm)

About 90% of torque goes into overcoming friction (thread and under the nut). Only 10% stretches the bolt. That's why lubrication changes the result so much.

Typical VDI 2230 table values for 90% use of yield strength with μ = 0.12. Dry or plated bolts need more torque for the same preload; moly paste needs less. Equipment manuals and gasketed joints use their own values.

Torque tools

  • Click wrench: set, pull smoothly at 90° to the handle until click. Wind back to the lowest setting after use (not zero below the scale).
  • Use within 20–100% of the wrench range for accuracy (±4% typical, ISO 6789).
  • Torque multiplier: gear ratio; the reaction arm must rest on a solid point.
  • Hydraulic torque wrench: square drive or low profile cassette. Pressure-to-torque chart for each tool. Reaction point must be secure.
  • Torque-angle method: snug, then turn a set angle. More consistent preload.
  • Direct tension indicators (DTI washers) and ultrasonic bolt elongation measurement for critical joints.

Calibration

Calibrate torque wrenches every 12 months or 5,000 cycles (ISO 6789), or after a drop. Hydraulic pumps and gauges as a set. Record tool serial numbers on critical joints.

Extension adapters

An adapter (crow's foot) that extends the wrench length changes the actual torque:

Wrench setting = T × L ÷ (L + E)

L = wrench length to the drive centre, E = extension length. A crow's foot at 90° to the handle needs no correction.
