Lathe work

Lathe parts, cutting speeds, tooling, setting up, turning, facing, boring, threading and safety.

On this page

Cutting speed and rpm

rpm = (Cutting speed m/min × 1000) ÷ (π × Diameter mm)
MaterialHSS (m/min)Carbide (m/min)
Mild steel25–35120–250
Medium carbon / alloy steel15–2590–180
Stainless (austenitic)10–2080–150
Cast iron15–2580–150
Brass / bronze40–90150–300
Aluminium60–150200–600

Example: 80 mm mild steel bar, carbide at 150 m/min: rpm = 150,000 ÷ (3.14 × 80) ≈ 600 rpm.

Set-up

  • Three-jaw chuck: quick, self-centring, ±0.05 mm typical runout.
  • Four-jaw independent: clock true to 0.01 mm. Best for repair work on shafts.
  • Between centres: for long shafts. Use a steady rest for long thin work.
  • Tool height on centre exactly. Below centre rubs, above centre digs.
  • Keep tool overhang short.

Operations

  • Facing: from outside to centre (or centre out for finishing).
  • Turning: rough with heavy cuts, finish with light cut (0.1–0.3 mm), fine feed.
  • Boring: largest boring bar that fits, shortest overhang.
  • Threading: set the gearbox for pitch, set compound to 29–30° for 60° threads, use the thread dial for re-engagement, check with a thread gauge or the mating part.
  • Parting: tool exactly on centre, slow speed, plenty of coolant, close to the chuck.

Common shaft repairs

  • Worn bearing seat: metal spray or welding and re-machine, or sleeve (machine undersize, shrink on a sleeve, finish to size). Check with the engineer for critical shafts.
  • Damaged threads: recut if enough material, else build up and recut.
  • Straightening bent shafts: by press or controlled heat, only with engineering approval for critical rotating shafts.
  • Always check final runout between centres.

Safety

  • No gloves near the rotating chuck (entanglement). No loose sleeves, rings, long hair.
  • Remove the chuck key every time. Never leave it in.
  • Guards in place, e-stop tested.
  • Use a brush or hook for swarf, never hands.
