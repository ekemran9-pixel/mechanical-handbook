Reading a metric vernier (0.02 mm)

  1. Read the main scale mm to the left of the vernier zero, e.g. 23 mm.
  2. Find the vernier line that lines up exactly with a main scale line, e.g. line 17.
  3. Multiply by 0.02: 17 × 0.02 = 0.34.
  4. Reading = 23.34 mm.

Reading a metric micrometer (0.01 mm)

  1. Read the sleeve: whole mm on the upper line, plus 0.5 mm if a lower half-line is visible past the last mm mark. E.g. 12.5.
  2. Read the thimble line on the sleeve centre line, e.g. 28 → 0.28.
  3. Total = 12.5 + 0.28 = 12.78 mm.
  4. Use the ratchet for consistent force. Check zero with the standard before use.

Measuring a shaft or bore properly

  • Measure at two positions along the length and at two angles 90° apart (four readings). That shows taper and ovality.
  • Let parts reach room temperature. Steel grows 0.0117 mm per metre per °C: a 100 mm shaft at 30 °C above ambient reads 0.035 mm big.
  • Rock a bore gauge to find the minimum reading (true diameter), and the inside mic to find the maximum across a bore.

Dial indicators

  • Set the plunger at right angles to the surface. Preload a quarter to half a turn.
  • Use a rigid magnetic base and short arms.
  • Lever-type (test) indicators read accurately only when the stylus is nearly parallel to the surface; angle errors are large otherwise (cosine error).

Feeler gauges

  • A light drag means the right size. Don't force.
  • Stack no more than 3 leaves. Clean them; oily leaves read thicker.

Care and calibration

  • Keep in cases, clean and lightly oiled. Never drop.
  • Calibrate on a schedule (often 6–12 months) with a sticker showing due date. Don't use out-of-date tools on QC-recorded measurements.
  • Check micrometers against standards and dial indicators against a calibrator.
