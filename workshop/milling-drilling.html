Milling, drilling & tapping

Milling basics, keyway cutting, drill sizes, tap drill table, reaming and drilling safety.

On this page
  1. Milling

  3. Metric tap drills (coarse threads)
  4. UNC tap drills

Milling

  • rpm formula as for the lathe, using cutter diameter.
  • Feed (mm/min) = feed per tooth × number of teeth × rpm. Typical feed per tooth 0.05–0.15 mm for end mills in steel.
  • Climb milling (cutter rotation with the feed): better finish, needs a rigid machine without backlash. Conventional: safer on manual machines with backlash.
  • Clamp work rigidly in a vice with parallels. Clock the vice jaw square.

Keyways

  • Shaft keyway: end mill or slot drill of the key width, depth per standard (DIN 6885 / BS 4235).
  • Hub keyway: broaching, slotting or keyseat cutter.
  • Keyway must be on centre within 0.02–0.05 mm. Check with a key and dial.

Tapping by hand

  1. Drill the right tap drill size. Chamfer the hole.
  2. Start with the taper (first) tap, square to the work. Check with a square.
  3. Turn half a turn forward, quarter turn back to break the chip. Use cutting oil.
  4. Finish with the plug, then bottoming tap for blind holes.
  5. Broken tap: tap extractor, or EDM. Don't drill it (it's harder than the drill).

Drilling

  • Centre punch, then pilot drill for holes over about 10 mm.
  • Clamp the work. Never hold by hand on a drill press: a grabbed part spins and cuts.
  • Slow down for large drills and hard materials. Steady feed, clear chips.
  • Ream for accurate holes: drill 0.2–0.5 mm under, ream at half the drilling speed.
