Threads & identification

Metric, UNC/UNF, BSW, BSP, NPT and other threads: how to identify them and thread gauge use.

On this page
  1. Identify a thread
  2. Common thread systems
  3. Watch out

Identify a thread

  1. Measure the major diameter (outside of a bolt, or the tap size for a hole) with a caliper.
  2. Measure pitch with a thread pitch gauge (mm) or count threads per inch (TPI).
  3. Check the thread angle: 60° (metric, UN, NPT) or 55° (Whitworth, BSP). The gauge fits snug on the right one.
  4. Check for taper (NPT, BSPT): the diameter changes along the length.

Watch out

  • 1/2" BSP and 1/2" NPT both have 14 TPI but different angles. They seem to fit but leak.
  • Metric M10 × 1.5 vs 3/8"-16 UNC: close but not the same. Never force.
  • Left-hand threads: marked LH, a groove on the nut flats, or notches. Found on impeller nuts, gas fittings (fuel gas regulators), bicycle pedals.
  • Gas cylinder valves often use special threads (e.g. 5/8" BSP LH for acetylene in the UK).
