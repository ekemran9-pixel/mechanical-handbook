Hand & power tools

Choosing and using spanners, sockets, hammers, pullers, grinders, impact wrenches and the inspection checks.

On this page

  2. Hammers
  3. Pullers
  4. Angle grinders
  5. Impact wrenches
  6. Magnetic base drills
  7. Tool inspection

Spanners and sockets

  • Use a ring spanner or 6-point socket over open-end where possible. Open ends round off nuts.
  • Pull, don't push. If you must push, use an open palm.
  • Never add pipe extensions ("cheater bars") to spanners. Use a torque multiplier or the right tool.
  • Impact sockets (black, thick wall) only on impact wrenches. Chrome sockets shatter.
  • Slugging (striking) spanners: use with a proper hammer and hand guard holder, never hold the spanner by hand while striking.

Hammers

  • Soft-faced (copper, brass, nylon, dead-blow) for machined parts.
  • Never strike two hardened faces together (chips fly).
  • Check heads are tight and handles uncracked. Mushroomed chisels and punches: grind off the burr.

Pullers

  • Two-jaw or three-jaw, hydraulic for heavy work. Jaws must grip square behind the part.
  • Wrap a blanket or puller safety cover around the puller: parts and jaws can fly off under load.
  • Back-plate (bearing separator) pullers for bearings tight on shafts.

Angle grinders

  • Disc rated rpm above the grinder rpm. Check the disc expiry date (resin bonded discs typically 3 years).
  • Guard on and positioned between you and the disc.
  • Two hands, side handle fitted. Stand out of line of the disc.
  • Cutting discs are for cutting only. Grinding with the side shatters them.
  • Let it reach speed before contact. Never lay it down still spinning.
  • Face shield over safety glasses, hearing protection.

Impact wrenches

  • Fast for removal, but not for final torque on critical bolts. Run down and then torque with a calibrated tool.
  • Check air hose whip checks and couplings.

Magnetic base drills

  • Clean, flat, thick enough steel (often > 10 mm) for full magnet strength.
  • Always use the safety chain/strap, especially on vertical or overhead work.
  • Annular cutters: use cutting fluid, pilot pin, steady feed.

Tool inspection

  • Monthly or quarterly colour coding (e.g. tape colour of the quarter) shows a tool was inspected.
  • Electrical tools: PAT/test tag, cable and plug intact, RCD/GFCI used.
  • Tether tools when working at height.
  • Remove damaged tools from service and tag them.
