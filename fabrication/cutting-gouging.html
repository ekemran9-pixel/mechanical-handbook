Cutting & gouging

Oxy-fuel, plasma, air-carbon arc gouging, mechanical cutting and bevelling, with settings and safety.

On this page
  1. Oxy-fuel cutting
  2. Plasma cutting
  3. Air carbon arc gouging (arc-air)
  4. Mechanical cutting and bevelling

Oxy-fuel cutting

Preheat flame heats steel to ignition (about 870 °C, bright red). A jet of pure oxygen burns the iron. Works only on carbon and low alloy steel (not stainless, aluminium, cast iron).

Figures vary a lot by nozzle maker and fuel. Use the nozzle chart.

  • Acetylene: hottest flame, fastest preheat. Max working pressure 1 bar (15 psi).
  • Propane: cheaper, slower preheat, more oxygen used. Needs propane-type nozzles.
  • Good cut: square edge, slight drag lines, sharp top edge, little slag that knocks off easily.
  • Rounded top edge: preheat too strong or torch too close. Heavy slag underneath: speed too slow or too fast. Bevelled cut: torch not square.

Plasma cutting

  • Cuts any electrically conductive metal: carbon steel, stainless, aluminium, copper.
  • Air plasma common for up to about 25–40 mm (hand-held). Mechanised with O₂ or N₂/H₂ mixes for quality cuts.
  • Narrow kerf, small HAZ, fast on thin and medium plate.
  • Consumables: electrode and nozzle wear. Replace both together when the cut quality drops.
  • Dry, clean air supply essential.
  • Eye protection: shade 8–11 depending on current.

Air carbon arc gouging (arc-air)

  • A copper-coated carbon electrode makes an arc; compressed air (5.5–7 bar) blows the molten metal away.
  • Used for back gouging, removing defects, removing welds.
  • DCEP, high current (e.g. 9.5 mm rod at 350–450 A).
  • Electrode angle about 35–45°, air jet behind the arc on the cutting side.
  • Grind 1–2 mm after gouging carbon steel to remove the carbon-enriched layer before rewelding. On stainless, avoid or grind more.
  • Very loud (over 100 dB) and lots of molten spray: hearing and fire protection, clear the area.

Mechanical cutting and bevelling

  • Pipe bevelling machines (clamshell / split frame, ID-mounted): best bevel quality for alloy and stainless.
  • Band saws: square cuts, all materials.
  • Abrasive cut-off discs: quick, watch disc rating and guard.
  • Wheel pipe cutters: small bore, leave a burr inside to remove.
  • Thermal cutting of alloy steels (Cr-Mo, P91) usually needs preheat and removal of 1.5–3 mm of the HAZ by grinding or machining afterwards.
