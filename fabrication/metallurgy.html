Steel metallurgy for welders

Carbon equivalent

CE (IIW) = C + Mn/6 + (Cr + Mo + V)/5 + (Ni + Cu)/15
  • CE < 0.40: good weldability, little or no preheat on moderate thickness.
  • CE 0.40–0.50: preheat needed, especially thick.
  • CE > 0.50: preheat, low-H, control cooling, maybe PWHT.

Pcm (Ito-Bessyo) is used for low carbon, modern steels and pipeline: Pcm = C + Si/30 + (Mn + Cu + Cr)/20 + Ni/60 + Mo/15 + V/10 + 5B.

Heat-affected zone (HAZ)

The base metal next to the weld that was heated but not melted. Its grain structure changes. Fast cooling of hardenable steel forms martensite: hard and brittle. HAZ hardness limits are often 248 HV10 for sour service (NACE MR0175/ISO 15156 at 22 HRC) and 325–350 HV for general service.

Hydrogen (cold) cracking

Needs three things together: hydrogen, a hard microstructure and stress. Appears hours (up to 48 h) after welding, in the HAZ or weld metal. Remove one factor:

  • Low-hydrogen consumables, dry and clean joints.
  • Preheat and interpass temperature to slow cooling and let hydrogen escape.
  • Post-heat (hydrogen bake-out, e.g. 200–300 °C for 2 h) straight after welding on critical joints.
  • Reduce restraint, good fit-up.
  • Delay final NDT (often 24–48 h) on susceptible steels.

Hot (solidification) cracking

  • Centreline cracks during solidification. Caused by sulphur and phosphorus, deep narrow beads, high restraint.
  • Austenitic stainless fillers contain some ferrite (FN 3–10) to resist it.
  • Fix: bead shape width ≥ depth, fill craters, clean base metal.

Lamellar tearing

Step-like cracks in plate under T-joints loaded through thickness. Use Z-grade plate (through-thickness tested), buttering, joint design that spreads load.

Stainless problems

  • Sensitisation: chromium carbides form at 450–850 °C, leaving chromium-poor zones that corrode (weld decay). Use L grades (≤ 0.03% C) or stabilised (321, 347). Keep heat input and interpass low.
  • Sigma phase: brittle phase from long time at 600–900 °C.
  • Distortion: stainless expands 50% more than carbon steel and conducts heat poorly.
  • Contamination: carbon steel tools and grinding discs leave iron that rusts. Use stainless-only brushes and discs.
