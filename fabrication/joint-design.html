Joint design & fit-up

Joint types, groove preparations, bevel angles, root gaps, backing, tacking and fit-up tolerances for plate and pipe.

On this page

Five basic joints

  • Butt: pieces in the same plane, edge to edge.
  • Tee: one piece at right angles to the other's surface.
  • Corner: pieces at right angles meeting at edges.
  • Lap: overlapping pieces.
  • Edge: parallel pieces welded along edges.

Root gap and land

  • Root gap usually 1.6–3.2 mm per WPS. Too small: lack of penetration. Too big: burn-through, excess penetration.
  • Land (root face) 0–2 mm. Feather edge (no land) for some GTAW roots.

Backing

  • Steel backing strip: permanent, common in structural. Must be continuous and fused. Not used in piping where it causes crevice corrosion and flow obstruction.
  • Ceramic backing: removable, gives a shaped root. Good for one-sided plate welds.
  • Copper backing: chill bar, removable.
  • Consumable inserts (EB rings): in pipe, melted into the root.
  • Gas backing: purge for stainless.

Fit-up tolerances

  • Hi-lo (internal misalignment) on pipe: usually ≤ 1.6 mm or per WPS / code. Larger: counterbore or taper (1:4) the thicker side.
  • Plate misalignment (AWS D1.1): 10% of thinner part, max 3 mm.
  • Fillet weld fit-up gap: up to 1.6 mm OK; 1.6–5 mm add the gap to the leg size (D1.1).
  • Check pipe squareness: ≤ 1.6 mm out of square on end cuts for most sizes.
  • Flange alignment before welding: face square to pipe within 1/2° (or 0.5 mm per 100 mm), bolt holes straddling centre lines (two-holed) unless drawing says otherwise.

Tack welds

  • Use the same WPS, filler and preheat as the root.
  • Tackers must be qualified for the process.
  • Pipe: 3–4 tacks equally spaced for up to 6", more for larger. Length about 2–3× wall, min 12–25 mm.
  • Grind tack ends to a feather so the root ties in. Grind out cracked tacks completely.
  • Bridge tacks (on top of the bevel using bars) avoid root tacks in critical work. Remove them carefully and grind/inspect the area.
