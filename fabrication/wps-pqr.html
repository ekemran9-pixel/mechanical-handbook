WPS, PQR & WPQ

What each welding document is, essential variables, how to read a WPS and how to keep welding records.

pWPSPreliminary WPS: the proposed recipe before testingWelding engineer

Codes

  • ASME Section IX: procedure and welder qualification for ASME pressure work (boilers, vessels, B31 piping).
  • AWS D1.1: structural steel. Has prequalified WPSs that don't need testing if all the rules are followed.
  • ISO 15614-1 (procedures) and ISO 9606-1 (welders) in Europe and many other regions.
  • API 1104: pipelines.

What a WPS shows

  • Process(es), type (manual, semi-auto).
  • Joint design: groove type, angle, root gap, land, backing.
  • Base metals: P-numbers / groups, thickness range, diameter range.
  • Filler metals: specification (SFA), classification, F-number, A-number, size.
  • Positions and progression.
  • Preheat min, interpass max.
  • PWHT: temperature and time range, or none.
  • Gas: type, flow, backing gas.
  • Electrical: current type and polarity, amps, volts, travel speed, heat input range.
  • Technique: stringer or weave, cleaning method, back gouging, peening.

Essential variables

Changing an essential variable beyond its range needs a new PQR. Changing a supplementary essential variable needs a new PQR only where toughness (impact) testing is required. Non-essential variables can change by revising the WPS.

Typical essential variables (ASME IX)
Process change
Base metal P-number change (outside allowed groupings)
Thickness outside qualified range
Filler F-number or A-number change
Preheat decrease more than 55 °C (100 °F)
PWHT added, removed, or changed temperature range
Shielding gas change (GTAW/GMAW) in some cases
Heat input increase (when impact tested) — supplementary

Heat input

Heat input (kJ/mm) = (Volts × Amps × 60) ÷ (Travel speed mm/min × 1000)

ISO adds a thermal efficiency factor k (0.8 for SMAW/GMAW/FCAW, 0.6 for GTAW, 1.0 for SAW). Example: 24 V, 140 A, 150 mm/min = 1.34 kJ/mm (arc energy).

Foreman's job

  • Make sure every welder has the right WPS at the joint and is qualified for it (process, position, thickness, diameter, F-number).
  • Check preheat and interpass with a contact thermometer or crayons.
  • Check consumables match the WPS and are stored properly.
  • Keep the weld log (weld map): joint number, welder stamp, WPS, date, NDT required and result, repair number.
  • Welders stamp or mark their ID next to each weld (low-stress stamps or paint marker, per spec).
