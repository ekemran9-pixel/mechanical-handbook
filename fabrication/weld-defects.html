Weld defects & repairs

Every common weld imperfection, why it happens, how to prevent it, and how to repair it.

On this page
  1. Defect, discontinuity, imperfection
  2. Surface defects
  3. Internal defects
  4. Repair procedure

Defect, discontinuity, imperfection

A discontinuity is any break in the weld. It becomes a defect (rejectable) only when it exceeds the code's acceptance criteria. So "porosity" might be acceptable in one code and rejectable in another.

Repair procedure

  1. Locate the defect exactly from NDT (UT gives depth; RT gives length and position).
  2. Mark the area. Excavate by grinding or air-arc gouging (then grind to bright metal, 1–2 mm beyond the gouged surface).
  3. Shape the excavation into a groove with sloping ends (at least 3:1 at the ends) for access.
  4. Confirm removal by MT or PT on the excavation (always for cracks).
  5. Preheat per WPS (repairs often need higher preheat due to restraint).
  6. Reweld using the qualified repair WPS.
  7. Re-inspect with the original method plus any extra required (often 100% of the repair plus a margin).
  8. Log the repair: weld number, R1 (first repair), welder, date. Many specs limit repairs to the same area to two attempts (R2); a third needs engineering approval.
Track your weld reject rate. Rejects ÷ welds tested (or by length). Most projects aim for under 2–3% by joint. A welder trending above that needs coaching or retesting.
