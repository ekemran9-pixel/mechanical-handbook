Welding aluminium

Aluminium properties, oxide removal, GTAW AC and GMAW pulse, preheat limits and common problems.

On this page
  1. GTAW
  2. GMAW
  3. Strength loss
  4. Cracking
  • Melts at about 660 °C, but its oxide melts at about 2,050 °C. The oxide must be removed: stainless brush (aluminium only) and solvent just before welding.
  • Conducts heat about 4–5× faster than steel: needs more current, and the weld can suddenly sink through.
  • No colour change before melting.
  • Porosity comes from hydrogen: moisture, oil, damp filler. Keep rods sealed and clean.

GTAW

  • AC with balance control: more electrode-positive time for cleaning, more negative for penetration (typical 65–75% EN).
  • Pure, zirconiated or lanthanated tungsten.
  • Argon, or argon/helium for thicker sections.

GMAW

  • Spray or pulsed spray, 100% argon (Ar/He for thick).
  • Soft wire: use push-pull or spool gun, U-groove rolls, PTFE liner, contact tip oversize.
  • Push technique (forehand) 10–15°.

Strength loss

Heat-treatable alloys (6061-T6) lose up to 30–40% strength in the HAZ. Design must allow for it. Keep preheat and interpass low (often ≤ 120 °C, max 150 °C). Don't hold at temperature.

Cracking

  • 6xxx alloys are crack sensitive without filler. Always add filler (4043 or 5356) to change the chemistry.
  • Fill craters at the end of each weld; use crater fill function.
