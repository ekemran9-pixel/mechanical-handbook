Welding & cutting safety

Fumes, UV, electric shock, fire, gas cylinders, confined spaces and the PPE for welders and fitters.

On this page
  1. Fumes
  2. Arc radiation
  3. Electric shock
  4. Fire
  5. Gas cylinders
  6. PPE checklist

Fumes

  • All welding fume is a carcinogen (IARC Group 1). Use local exhaust ventilation (LEV) at the arc, or fume extraction guns.
  • Stainless welding fume contains hexavalent chromium and nickel. Use LEV and respiratory protection (powered air respirator).
  • Galvanised steel: zinc fume causes metal fume fever. Remove the coating or extract and protect.
  • Manganese from many electrodes: neurological damage over long exposure.
  • Degreaser vapour (chlorinated solvents) near the arc can form phosgene. Keep apart.

  • Welding screens to protect others from flash.
  • Cover skin: UV burns like sunburn in minutes.
  • Arc eye: gritty, painful eyes hours after exposure. Get first aid.

Electric shock

  • Inspect leads and holders daily. Replace damaged insulation.
  • Dry gloves. Don't stand in water. Use insulated mats in wet locations.
  • Use voltage reduction devices (VRD) in confined or wet spaces.
  • Earth (return) clamp close to the weld, on clean metal. Never earth through bearings, chains or pipe racks carrying others' work.
  • Switch off and remove rods when leaving the station.

Fire

  • Hot work permit and gas test.
  • Clear combustibles within 11 m (35 ft) or cover with fire blankets.
  • Fire watch during work and at least 30 minutes after (often 60 min on some sites).
  • Cover drains, openings and gratings where sparks can fall.
  • Extinguisher at hand.

Gas cylinders

  • Store upright, chained, valve caps on, in ventilated areas.
  • Separate oxygen from fuel gases by 6 m or a 1.5 m high fire wall (30 min rated).
  • Flashback arrestors at the regulator outlets on both oxygen and fuel. Check valves at the torch.
  • Leak test with leak detection fluid, never a flame.
  • Open cylinder valves slowly, stand to the side of the regulator.
  • Acetylene cylinder valves open max 1½ turns, keep the key on the valve.
  • Never use oxygen to blow dust off or to ventilate. Oxygen-enriched clothing burns violently.
  • Close cylinders and bleed hoses at the end of the shift.

PPE checklist

  • Helmet with correct shade (auto-darkening checked), safety glasses under it.
  • Flame-resistant clothing, leather jacket or sleeves for overhead.
  • Leather welding gloves (TIG gloves thinner).
  • Safety boots with no exposed laces; spats for heavy spatter.
  • Hearing protection for gouging and grinding.
  • Respiratory protection as the fume assessment requires.
