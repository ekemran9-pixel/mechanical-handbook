SAW (submerged arc)

Submerged arc equipment, wire/flux combinations, settings and flux handling.

On this page
  1. Wire and flux classification (AWS A5.17)
  2. Settings
  3. Flux handling
  4. Defects
  • Arc buried under granular flux. No visible arc, no spatter, very high deposition.
  • Used on long straight seams and circumferential seams on rotating vessels or pipe (with positioners or rollers).
  • Tractor, column-and-boom, or pipe mill set-ups. Tandem and twin wire for higher output.

Wire and flux classification (AWS A5.17)

F7A2-EM12K: flux + wire gives 70 ksi tensile (7), as welded (A), impact 27 J at −20 °F (2), with wire EM12K (medium manganese, silicon killed).

  • Neutral fluxes for multi-pass. Active fluxes add Mn/Si: fine for 1–2 passes only.
  • Agglomerated vs fused fluxes: agglomerated absorbs moisture faster.

Settings

  • Typical 3.2–4.0 mm wire at 400–700 A, 28–36 V, 300–600 mm/min.
  • Flux depth just enough to cover the arc (about 25–30 mm).
  • On circumferential seams, place the wire slightly before top dead centre (against rotation) so the pool solidifies at the top.

Flux handling

  • Store dry. Bake damp agglomerated flux per maker (often 300–350 °C, 2 h) and hold at 150 °C.
  • Sieve recycled flux and remove fines and slag. Mix with new flux.
  • Fused slag must be removed between passes.

Defects

  • Centreline (solidification) cracks: deep narrow bead (depth/width ratio over 1). Lower current, increase voltage, slow down.
  • Porosity from damp flux or rust.
  • Slag entrapment at toe in groove welds.
