Cr-Mo, P91 & dissimilar welds

Welding creep-resistant chrome-moly steels and joining dissimilar metals safely.

On this page
  1. Chrome-moly steels (P11, P22, P5)
  2. P91 / Grade 91 (9Cr-1Mo-V)
  3. Dissimilar metal welds (DMW)
  4. PMI

Chrome-moly steels (P11, P22, P5)

  • Used in high temperature steam, hydrogen and refinery heater service.
  • Prone to hydrogen cracking: low-hydrogen consumables, controlled preheat, interpass, post-heat and PWHT.
  • Match filler chemistry to the base metal.
  • PWHT is almost always required. Hardness checks after.
  • Material verification (PMI with XRF) on every component: a carbon steel elbow in a P22 line fails quickly.

P91 / Grade 91 (9Cr-1Mo-V)

Creep strength enhanced ferritic steel. Excellent strength at 550–620 °C, but unforgiving.

  1. Preheat 200–250 °C. Maintain throughout.
  2. Interpass max typically 300–350 °C.
  3. After welding, cool to below about 100 °C (to complete martensite transformation) unless the procedure says hold, but not to ambient before PWHT if hydrogen risk. Follow the specific procedure.
  4. PWHT 730–775 °C promptly. Wrong PWHT (too high or too low) ruins creep strength.
  5. Hardness after PWHT typically 190–250 HB (spec dependent). Too soft is as bad as too hard.
  6. Ni + Mn in filler limited (e.g. ≤ 1.0–1.2%) to keep the transformation temperature above PWHT.

Dissimilar metal welds (DMW)

  • Carbon steel to stainless: 309L filler for low temperature service. Carbon migrates at high temperature, so above about 315–425 °C use nickel filler (ERNiCr-3).
  • Buttering: lay a layer of the right filler on one side (often the ferritic side), PWHT if needed, then make the closing weld without PWHT.
  • Dilution: the first pass mixes base and filler. Check the Schaeffler or WRC-1992 diagram for the resulting structure (aim for some ferrite in austenitic deposits, avoid martensite).
  • Thermal expansion mismatch causes fatigue at high temperature cycles. Place DMWs in low-stress locations.

PMI

Positive material identification by XRF or OES confirms alloy content of pipe, fittings, bolts and weld metal. Mandatory on alloy systems on most refinery projects (API RP 578).
