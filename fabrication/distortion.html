Distortion control

Why welds distort, the types of distortion, and practical ways to prevent and correct it.

On this page
  1. Types
  2. Design and planning
  3. Shop techniques
  4. Correcting distortion
  5. Flange and pipe distortion

Weld metal shrinks as it cools. The surrounding metal resists. The result is either distortion (movement) or residual stress (locked-in). Usually both.

Types

  • Transverse shrinkage: across the weld.
  • Longitudinal shrinkage: along the weld (causes bowing on long members).
  • Angular distortion: plates fold toward the weld side (single-sided V welds and fillets).
  • Bowing / camber: long beams curve.
  • Buckling: thin plates go wavy.
  • Rotational: gap closes or opens as the weld progresses.

Design and planning

  • Don't overweld. A 6 mm fillet where 8 mm is welded puts in 78% more weld metal than needed.
  • Use intermittent welds where allowed.
  • Use double-sided joints (X prep) and balance welding about the neutral axis.
  • Place welds near the neutral axis.
  • Use fewer passes with larger beads (less angular distortion, but watch heat input limits).
  • Use J or U preps on thick plate (less weld metal).

Shop techniques

  • Presetting: set the parts slightly the opposite way so shrinkage pulls them square. Example: preset a T-joint flange 1–2° outward.
  • Back-to-back assembly: clamp two identical parts together and weld both.
  • Strongbacks and clamps: hold the joint. Release after cooling.
  • Backstep welding: weld short segments in the opposite direction to the overall progression.
  • Skip welding: weld short lengths spaced apart, then fill in.
  • Balanced sequence: alternate sides on double-V joints. On pipe flanges, weld opposite quadrants.
  • Peening (if allowed by the WPS): stretches the bead to counter shrinkage. Not on root or cap passes.

Correcting distortion

  • Mechanical: presses, jacks, clamps. Cold working may need engineering approval for pressure parts.
  • Flame (heat) straightening: heat spots, lines or wedges (V-heats) on the long side to make it shrink. Carbon steel: keep below about 650 °C (dull red). Q&T steels: lower limits, often 600 °C or less. Stainless: limited.
  • Let it cool naturally. Water quenching only where the material allows.

Flange and pipe distortion

  • Weld-neck flanges pull out of square during welding. Check face squareness with a flange square after root and hot pass and adjust sequence.
  • Slip-on flanges: weld the inside fillet and outside fillet alternately in quadrants.
  • Leave spools long on one end for final fit-up (field weld with extra length).
