Preheat, interpass & PWHT

Why and how to preheat, interpass control, post-heat, PWHT cycles, thermocouple placement and records.

Preheat

Preheat slows the cooling rate, so less hard martensite forms and hydrogen can diffuse out. It also drives off moisture.

  • Apply with gas torches (rosebud), electric resistance pads or induction coils.
  • Heat a band at least 75 mm (3") each side of the joint, or 4× thickness (some codes), through the full thickness.
  • Measure on the side opposite the heat source where possible, or wait 1 minute per 25 mm thickness after removing the torch (ISO 13916 rule).
  • Use contact thermometers, thermocouples or temperature indicating crayons. A crayon rated at the temperature melts when the steel reaches it.

Interpass temperature

  • Minimum interpass = minimum preheat. Don't let the joint cool below it until the weld is done (or post-heated).
  • Maximum interpass protects toughness and corrosion resistance: often 250–315 °C for carbon steel, 150–175 °C for stainless, 100–150 °C for duplex.

Post-heat (dehydrogenation)

Right after welding, before the joint cools, raise it to around 200–300 °C and hold for 1–4 hours (by procedure). Common on thick Cr-Mo and high-restraint joints that can't go straight into PWHT.

PWHT (post weld heat treatment)

Heating the completed weld to below the lower critical temperature to relieve residual stresses and temper the HAZ.

Heating and cooling rates

  • Above 315–425 °C, control heating and cooling. A common rule: max rate (°C/h) = 220 × 25 ÷ thickness (mm), not more than 220 °C/h and not less than 55 °C/h.
  • Below the control temperature (often 300–425 °C) cool in still air under insulation.

Thermocouples and band widths

  • Soak band: weld width plus at least 1 t (thickness) or 25 mm each side, whichever is less (B31.3 says weld plus 25 mm each side minimum; AWS D10.10 gives more detail).
  • Heated band and insulation band wider (insulation band often soak band + 4 × √(R t)).
  • Attach thermocouples by capacitor discharge welding. Usually at least 2 per joint (top and bottom for horizontal pipe), more for large pipe.
  • Record the full chart: heating, soak, cooling, with time scale and thermocouple ID.

Before PWHT

  • All welding, including attachments, finished. Welding after PWHT means doing it again.
  • NDT done if required before PWHT.
  • Support the piping: strength drops at temperature and the pipe can sag.
  • Remove valves, instruments and soft materials from the heat zone.
  • Open ends closed to stop chimney effect (draught cooling the inside).

After PWHT

  • Hardness test (often required: e.g. ≤ 225 HB for P4/P5, ≤ 241 HB for some Cr-Mo, ≤ 200 HB for sour carbon steel).
  • Final NDT if specified after PWHT.
  • Attach the chart to the weld record.
