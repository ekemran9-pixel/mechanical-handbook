Welding stainless & duplex

Practical rules for austenitic, duplex and martensitic stainless steels: cleanliness, heat input, purging and pickling.

On this page
  1. Keep it clean
  2. Austenitic (304, 316, 321)
  3. Duplex (2205) and super duplex (2507)
  4. Martensitic (410, 420)
  5. After welding: pickling and passivation

Keep it clean

  • Separate stainless fabrication from carbon steel. Free iron from carbon steel tools rusts on stainless.
  • Use stainless wire brushes and dedicated grinding discs (iron-free, labelled for stainless).
  • No carbon steel slings or chains; use nylon or protect with plastic.
  • Clean with acetone or chloride-free solvent. No marker pens with chlorides or sulphur.
  • Store on wooden or plastic racks, not steel.

Austenitic (304, 316, 321)

  • No preheat. Max interpass about 150–175 °C.
  • Low heat input: stringer beads, fast travel.
  • Back purge the root (argon) for GTAW roots, or use flux-cored backing products.
  • Use L-grade fillers for corrosion resistance.
  • Expect distortion: tack closer, clamp, balance sequence.

Duplex (2205) and super duplex (2507)

  • Target ferrite 30–65% (ferrite scope test on weld and HAZ).
  • Heat input usually 0.5–2.5 kJ/mm for 2205 (0.2–1.5 for 2507). Too low: too much ferrite. Too high: intermetallic phases.
  • Interpass max typically 150 °C (2205) and 100 °C (2507).
  • Use over-alloyed filler (2209, 2594) for the right phase balance.
  • Purge with argon or argon + 2% nitrogen; O₂ < 50 ppm typical.
  • Corrosion test (ASTM G48) and impact tests often on the PQR.

Martensitic (410, 420)

  • Preheat 200–300 °C. PWHT required (often 650–760 °C).
  • Or use austenitic filler (309) for repairs where PWHT isn't possible (with engineering approval).

After welding: pickling and passivation

  • Remove heat tint with pickling paste (nitric/hydrofluoric acid) or electrochemical cleaning. Mechanical brushing alone leaves a weak layer.
  • Passivate (nitric or citric acid) to rebuild the protective chromium oxide layer.
  • Rinse with low-chloride water. Neutralise and dispose of acid waste correctly.
  • Pickling acids are very hazardous: full PPE (face shield, acid gloves, apron), with calcium gluconate gel available for HF exposure.
