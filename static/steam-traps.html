Steam traps

Trap types, how to test them, common faults and good installation practice.

On this page
  1. Types
  2. Testing
  3. Installation

A steam trap lets condensate and air out and holds steam in. Failed open traps waste steam. Failed closed traps flood equipment, causing water hammer, corrosion and poor heating.

Types

TypeHow it worksUse
Thermodynamic (TD)Disc snaps shut on flash steam velocitySteam mains drip legs, tracing. Small, robust
Float & thermostatic (F&T)Float opens on level; thermostatic element vents airHeat exchangers, process with modulating loads. Continuous discharge
Inverted bucketBucket floats when steam is under it, sinks with condensateMains, process, superheated (with check). Needs a water seal (prime)
Thermostatic (balanced pressure, bimetallic)Opens when condensate cools below steam temperatureTracing, startup air venting, where subcooled discharge is acceptable

Testing

  • Ultrasound: listen to the cycling. TD traps click; F&T discharge steadily; bucket traps cycle.
  • Temperature: inlet hot, outlet cooler. Inlet cold = blocked or failed closed (or no load).
  • Sight glass / test valve: open discharge to atmosphere downstream: flash steam (lazy, white) is normal; live steam (clear blue jet near the outlet) means blowing.

Installation

  • Drip legs on mains every 30–50 m and at low points, with pocket the same size as the main for small mains.
  • Strainer and isolation valves before the trap, check valve after when lifting condensate.
  • One trap per piece of equipment (no group trapping).
  • Install the trap below the drain point.
