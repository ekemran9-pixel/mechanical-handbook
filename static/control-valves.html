Control valves (mechanical)

Control valve parts, actuators, positioners, stroke checks, trim and packing work for the mechanical team.

  • Body: globe (most common), ball (segmented), butterfly, rotary plug.
  • Trim: plug, seat, cage. Special trims reduce noise and cavitation.
  • Actuator: spring and diaphragm (most common), piston, electric.
  • Fail action: fail open (FO), fail closed (FC), fail last (FL). Shown on the P&ID and nameplate.
  • Positioner: makes the valve go to the position asked by the 4–20 mA signal.

Mechanical work

  1. Isolate (block valves and bypass), depressure, drain. Isolate air supply and electrical signal.
  2. Release spring compression in the actuator before removing it, using the maker's method. Actuator springs store a lot of energy.
  3. Remove bonnet, check plug and seat for erosion, cavitation and wire-drawing.
  4. Replace trim as needed. Lap the plug to seat if metal seated.
  5. Replace packing (PTFE V-rings or graphite) in the right order.
  6. Reassemble. Bench set the actuator: spring range and stroke.
  7. Instrument tech calibrates the positioner (0, 25, 50, 75, 100% stroke check).

Cavitation vs flashing

  • Cavitation: pressure dips below vapour pressure in the vena contracta, then recovers above it. Bubbles collapse: noise and pitting damage downstream of the seat.
  • Flashing: outlet pressure stays below vapour pressure. Liquid turns to vapour. Smooth, polished erosion damage.
