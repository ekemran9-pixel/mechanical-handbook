Pressure vessels

Vessel parts, codes, nameplates, MAWP, inspection, manway opening and repair rules.

On this page
  1. Codes
  2. Nameplate
  3. Parts
  4. Inspection (API 510)
  5. Opening a manway
  6. Repairs

Codes

  • ASME Section VIII Div. 1: most vessels. Div. 2 for higher stress designs.
  • PED / EN 13445 in Europe.
  • API 510: in-service inspection, repair and alteration of vessels.
  • NBIC / National Board: repair rules ("R" stamp in the US).

Nameplate

Shows: maker, serial number, year, MAWP (maximum allowable working pressure) at temperature, MDMT (minimum design metal temperature), radiography extent (RT-1 to RT-4), PWHT ("HT"), code stamp and National Board number.

Parts

  • Shell, heads (2:1 ellipsoidal, hemispherical, torispherical, flat), nozzles with reinforcing pads, manways, skirt or saddles, internals.
  • Reinforcing pads have a tell-tale hole (threaded 1/4") for testing the pad welds. Leave it open (or plugged with grease only) in service so a leak shows.

Inspection (API 510)

  • External visual: corrosion under insulation (CUI), supports, foundation, nozzles, earth connections.
  • Internal visual: corrosion, erosion, cracks, lining condition, internals.
  • UT thickness at set CML (condition monitoring locations).
  • Corrosion rate = (previous thickness − current) ÷ years. Remaining life = (current − minimum required) ÷ rate.
  • Internal inspection interval: half the remaining life or 10 years, whichever is less.

Opening a manway

  1. Vessel isolated with blinds (not just valves), drained, depressured, purged, cooled.
  2. Gas test from the top first.
  3. Loosen bolts at the bottom of the manway first, so pressure or liquid behind it shows itself away from you.
  4. Keep 2 bolts loose but in at the top until the cover is free.
  5. Use a davit or chain block for the cover.
  6. Fit a confined space entry barrier and sign.

Repairs

Any weld on a pressure boundary is a repair or alteration under code. It needs an approved procedure, qualified welders, inspection (NDT), sometimes PWHT and a pressure test, and the authorised inspector's acceptance. Never weld on a vessel shell (even a bracket) without engineering approval.
