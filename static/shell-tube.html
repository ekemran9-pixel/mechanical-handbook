Shell & tube exchangers

TEMA types, parts, fixed tubesheet vs floating head vs U-tube, and how to read an exchanger data sheet.

On this page

  2. Common designs
  3. Parts

TEMA designation

Three letters: front head type, shell type, rear head type. Example: AES = removable channel and cover, one-pass shell, split-ring floating head.

Common designs

  • Fixed tubesheet (BEM, NEN): cheapest. Shell side can't be mechanically cleaned. Needs an expansion joint for big temperature differences.
  • U-tube (BEU, AEU): bundle removable, handles expansion. Tube insides hard to clean at the bends.
  • Floating head (AES, AET): bundle removable, both sides cleanable. Most common in refineries.

Parts

  • Shell, shell cover, channel, channel cover, pass partition plates.
  • Tubesheets (stationary and floating), tubes, baffles, tie rods and spacers, impingement plate.
  • Floating head cover, split backing ring.
  • Gaskets: channel cover, tubesheet (both sides), floating head, shell cover.

Which fluid goes where

  • Tube side: corrosive, fouling, high pressure or cooling water (easier to clean and cheaper to upgrade).
  • Shell side: viscous, condensing, low flow fluids.

Tube data

  • Common OD: 19.05 mm (¾") and 25.4 mm (1"). Wall in BWG: 14 BWG = 2.11 mm, 16 BWG = 1.65 mm, 18 BWG = 1.24 mm.
  • Pitch: triangular (more tubes, shell side hard to clean) or square / rotated square (cleanable lanes).
  • Tube to tubesheet joint: rolled (expanded), rolled and seal welded, or strength welded.
