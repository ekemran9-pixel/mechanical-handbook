Exchanger maintenance & testing

Opening, bundle pulling, cleaning, tube plugging, re-rolling, hydrotests and leak tests step by step.

On this page

  2. Cleaning
  3. Inspection
  4. Testing for leaks

  6. Re-rolling (re-expanding) tube joints

Opening sequence (AES type)

  1. Permit, isolate both sides with spades/blinds, drain and depressure. Gas free.
  2. Remove channel cover. Inspect pass partition gasket area.
  3. Remove shell cover, then floating head cover (split ring bolts).
  4. Remove channel if bundle is to be pulled.
  5. Attach the bundle extractor (or crane with spreader) and pull the bundle onto supports. Use pulling eyes or rods through the tubes as designed, never pull by the tubes alone.
  6. Place the bundle on timber saddles at the baffle positions.

Cleaning

  • Hydrojetting: 700–1,400 bar (up to 2,500 bar for hard deposits) for tube insides and the bundle outside. Only trained crews with dead-man controls and full PPE.
  • Chemical cleaning: for scale; circulate acid with inhibitor, neutralise, passivate.
  • Mechanical: brushes, scrapers, drills for hard plugs.
  • Pigging (projectiles shot through the tubes with water).

Inspection

  • Visual: tube ends, tubesheet face, baffles, gasket faces, corrosion.
  • Eddy current testing (ECT): non-ferrous tubes (copper alloys, stainless, titanium).
  • IRIS (internal rotary inspection system, ultrasonic): ferrous tubes, accurate wall measurement.
  • RFT (remote field testing): ferrous tubes, faster but less accurate.
  • Shell: UT thickness, internal visual.

Testing for leaks

Pneumatic and helium leak tests are used for very small leaks or where water isn't allowed.

Plugging tubes

  • Mark leaking tubes on a tubesheet map. Plug both ends.
  • Use tapered plugs of compatible material, driven in. Or explosive/expanding plugs for high pressure.
  • Seal welding of plugs only if the material allows.
  • Keep total plugged tubes within the design margin (often 5–10% of tubes) and record each one. The process engineer must approve more.

Re-rolling (re-expanding) tube joints

  • Use a controlled-torque roller expander. Typical wall reduction 4–8% for steel, 8–10% for non-ferrous.
  • Don't over-roll: it work-hardens and cracks the tube or loosens neighbours.
  • Roll from the tubesheet face inward, not beyond the tubesheet back face (stay 3 mm short).

Reassembly

  • New gaskets always. Clean faces, check for steam cuts and pass partition grooves.
  • Check pass partition gasket ribs line up.
  • Tighten in star pattern in stages (see flange bolting). Big covers use hydraulic tensioners.
  • Hot bolting (re-torquing after warm-up) only by procedure.
