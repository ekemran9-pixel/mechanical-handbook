Valve repair & testing

Overhauling isolation valves: lapping seats, repacking, bonnet gaskets and shop pressure testing to API 598.

On this page
  1. Overhaul steps (gate or globe)
  2. Shop pressure tests (API 598)
  3. In-line repairs
  4. Thermal binding and pressure locking

Overhaul steps (gate or globe)

  1. Record tag, size, class, material, trim. Check it's depressured and flushed.
  2. Open slightly to relieve body cavity (gate valves).
  3. Remove bonnet bolts, lift bonnet and stem out.
  4. Clean, inspect body, seats, disc/wedge, stem, back seat, guides.
  5. Lap seats and disc: lapping plate or tool with grinding compound (coarse then fine). For gate valves, check the wedge-to-seat contact with blue.
  6. Check stem straightness (≤ 0.05 mm per 300 mm) and finish where it passes the packing.
  7. New bonnet gasket. New packing (die-formed graphite rings with braided end rings).
  8. Reassemble. Tighten bonnet bolts in a cross pattern. Tighten packing gland to torque.
  9. Stroke the valve fully a few times.

Shop pressure tests (API 598)

TestPressureAcceptance
Shell (hydrostatic)1.5 × rated pressure at 38 °CNo visible leak through body, bonnet or joint
Backseat (where fitted)1.1 × ratedNo visible leak
Low-pressure closure (air/gas)4–7 barPer API 598 table (bubbles per minute)
High-pressure closure (hydrostatic)1.1 × ratedSoft seated: zero leak. Metal seated: allowed drops per minute per size

Minimum test durations depend on size: e.g. 15 s for NPS ≤ 2, 60 s for NPS 2½–6, 120 s for 8–12, 300 s for 14 and up (shell test).

Class 150 carbon steel (WCB) rated 19.6 bar → shell test about 29.4 bar, seat test about 21.6 bar.

In-line repairs

  • Packing leak on a live valve: tighten gland evenly, a little at a time. Live-loading kits fit on many valves.
  • Sealant injection (by specialist) for stem or body leaks.
  • Lubricated plug valves: inject sealant through the fitting to free and seal.
  • Never back off a bonnet nut on a pressurised valve.

Thermal binding and pressure locking

A gate valve closed hot can lock as it cools. Liquid trapped in the bonnet cavity of a closed gate valve can heat up and push the wedges against the seats (pressure locking). Fix with a cavity relief hole on the upstream side, or bypass, per design.
