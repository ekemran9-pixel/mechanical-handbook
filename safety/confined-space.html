Confined space entry

Defining confined spaces, preparation, gas testing, entry permit, attendant duties and rescue.

On this page
  1. What is a confined space
  2. Preparation
  3. Atmosphere limits (typical)
  4. Attendant (hole watch)
  5. During entry

What is a confined space

Large enough to enter, limited ways in and out, not designed for continuous occupancy, and with a foreseeable hazard (toxic or flammable atmosphere, low oxygen, engulfment, entrapment). Vessels, tanks, columns, pits, sewers, ducts, large pipes, boilers.

Preparation

  1. Positive isolation of all lines (blinds or spools removed). Electrical and mechanical isolation of agitators, heaters.
  2. Drain, depressure, purge (steam, water, nitrogen then air).
  3. Ventilate with air movers. Never use oxygen to ventilate.
  4. Gas test from outside at several levels (top, middle, bottom). Gases separate by density.
  5. Rescue plan and equipment at the entrance (tripod and winch, breathing apparatus, stretcher).
  6. Entry permit issued, entrants and attendant named and briefed.

Atmosphere limits (typical)

GasAcceptable for entry
Oxygen19.5–23.5% (some sites 20–21%)
Flammable (LEL)< 10% LEL (often 0% for entry; < 10% for hot work outside)
H₂S< 1 ppm (TLV-TWA 1 ppm, STEL 5 ppm)
CO< 25 ppm (TLV) / 35 ppm (OSHA ceiling varies)
Benzene and other toxicsBelow their exposure limits, specific tubes or PIDs

Attendant (hole watch)

  • Stays at the entrance the whole time. Never enters for rescue.
  • Keeps a log of who is inside.
  • Keeps in contact with entrants.
  • Orders evacuation on alarm, change in conditions, or any concern.
  • Raises the alarm and starts the rescue plan.

During entry

  • Continuous gas monitoring with personal monitors.
  • Low-voltage (24 V or less) or intrinsically safe lighting and tools inside metal vessels.
  • Air-line or SCBA if the atmosphere can't be made safe.
  • Manage hot work, coating and cleaning chemicals that change the atmosphere.
Most confined space deaths are would-be rescuers. Never go in after a collapsed person without breathing apparatus and the rescue team.
