Working at height

Fall prevention and fall arrest, harness inspection, anchor points, dropped objects and rescue.

On this page
  1. Hierarchy
  2. Harness and lanyard
  3. Rescue
  4. Dropped objects

Hierarchy

  1. Avoid work at height (do it at ground level).
  2. Prevent falls: guardrails, scaffolds, MEWPs.
  3. Arrest falls: harness and lanyard, safety nets.

Harness and lanyard

  • Inspect before each use: webbing cuts, burns, chemical damage, stitching, buckles, the fall indicator on shock absorbers.
  • Fit snug: leg straps tight (flat hand fits under), chest strap at mid-chest, dorsal D-ring between shoulder blades.
  • Use a double (twin) lanyard for 100% tie-off when moving.
  • Shock-absorbing lanyards need clearance: lanyard length (e.g. 1.8 m) + shock absorber deployment (up to 1.75 m) + harness stretch and body height (~1.5 m) + safety margin (1 m). About 6 m below the anchor. Use self-retracting lifelines (SRL) where clearance is less.
  • Anchor at or above the D-ring. Anchors rated 15 kN (EN 795) or 5,000 lb (OSHA) per person.

Rescue

A person hanging in a harness can lose consciousness within 15–30 minutes (suspension trauma). Every job with fall arrest needs a rescue plan and equipment that can get the person down quickly.

Dropped objects

  • Tether tools and tag lines on loose items above 2 m.
  • Toe boards, netting, barricade below the work area.
  • Secure items on scaffolds and in baskets. Remove loose items after work.
  • A 1 kg spanner dropped 10 m hits with enough energy to kill without a hard hat.
