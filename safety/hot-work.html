Hot work

Hot work hazards, permits, gas testing, fire watch, habitat (pressurised enclosure) and spark control.

On this page
  1. Preparation
  2. Fire watch
  3. Pressurised enclosures (habitats)
  • Hot work includes welding, cutting, grinding, gouging, brazing, heating and any spark-producing tools in a hazardous area.
  • Permit with gas test before start, and repeat at intervals or continuous monitoring.
  • Work only at 0% LEL in many sites (limit by site rules).

Preparation

  • Clear combustibles within 11 m (35 ft) or protect with fire blankets.
  • Cover drains, sewers, cable trenches and openings within the spark zone. Wet down the area if suitable.
  • Check the other side of walls, floors and pipes (heat travels through steel).
  • Isolate and purge the equipment being worked on. Equipment that held flammables: clean and gas free inside, or inert.
  • Fire extinguisher (and hose if required) at hand.

Fire watch

  • Trained, with an extinguisher and a radio. No other duties.
  • Watches for sparks, smouldering and smoke during work and for at least 30 minutes after (often 60 min).
  • Has the authority to stop work.

Pressurised enclosures (habitats)

Used to weld in live plant areas. A fire-retardant enclosure is kept at positive pressure with clean air from a safe area. Gas detectors at the air intake and inside shut down the hot work and alarm if gas is detected. Requires a specialist provider and a dedicated procedure.
