Noise, vibration, heat & fatigue

Managing noise, hand-arm vibration, heat stress, cold, fatigue and manual handling on site.

On this page
  1. Noise
  2. Hand-arm vibration
  3. Heat stress
  4. Fatigue
  5. Manual handling

Noise

  • Hearing protection above 85 dB(A) average over 8 h. Every +3 dB doubles the energy: 88 dB allows 4 h, 91 dB 2 h (3 dB exchange rate).
  • If you need to shout to be heard 1 m away, it's over 85 dB.
  • Typical: angle grinder 100–105 dB, air arc gouging 110–120 dB, hydrojetting 110+ dB, steam blowing 130+ dB.

Hand-arm vibration

  • Grinders, impact wrenches, needle guns, chipping hammers.
  • Causes white finger and nerve damage. Limit exposure time (use the tool's m/s² rating and an exposure calculator: EU action value 2.5 m/s² A(8), limit 5 m/s²).
  • Rotate tasks, use low-vibration tools, keep hands warm.

Heat stress

  • Signs of heat exhaustion: heavy sweating, dizziness, nausea, headache, cramps. Move to shade, cool, drink water.
  • Heat stroke: confusion, hot dry skin, collapse. Medical emergency. Cool immediately with water and call for help.
  • Drink about 250 mL every 15–20 minutes in hot work. Don't wait for thirst.
  • Work-rest cycles by WBGT index. Acclimatise new workers over 1–2 weeks.
  • Extra care with chemical suits and inside vessels.

Fatigue

  • Long shifts (12 h) and night work raise error rates.
  • Limit consecutive shifts and overtime as site rules say. Watch the crew in the last hours of a shift and the early morning hours.
  • Don't schedule critical tasks (lifts, line breaks) at the end of a long shift.

Manual handling

  • Plan the lift. Test the weight. Above 25 kg (male guide, less for women and awkward loads) use help or lifting aids.
  • Feet apart, bend the knees, keep the back straight, hold close, no twisting.
  • Use trolleys, pallet trucks, chain blocks, vacuum lifters.
