Permit to work

How a permit-to-work system works, permit types, the foreman's responsibilities and handover rules.

On this page
  1. Common permit types
  2. Roles
  3. Foreman's checklist
  4. Handover
  5. SIMOPS

A permit to work (PTW) is a written system that controls hazardous work. It makes sure the hazards are known, the plant is safe, the controls are in place, and everyone involved knows what is happening.

Common permit types

  • Cold work: general maintenance with no ignition sources.
  • Hot work: welding, cutting, grinding, open flames, non-intrinsically-safe tools in hazardous areas.
  • Confined space entry.
  • Electrical / isolation certificate.
  • Excavation, working at height, radiography, lifting (on some sites).
  • Supporting certificates: isolation certificate, gas test certificate, LOTO register.

Roles

  • Issuing authority (IA): usually operations. Prepares the plant, specifies controls, issues the permit.
  • Performing authority (PA) / permit receiver: usually the foreman or supervisor. Accepts the permit, briefs the crew, makes sure conditions are followed.
  • Area authority: owns the area, approves.
  • Gas tester: authorised and trained.

Foreman's checklist

  1. Read the permit and all attached certificates (isolation, gas test, JSA).
  2. Walk the job with the issuer. Check isolations with your own eyes, apply your lock.
  3. Brief every crew member. Each one signs on.
  4. Display the permit at the worksite (or as site rules say).
  5. Make sure the work stays within the permit scope. New work = new permit or amendment.
  6. Stop work if conditions change (alarm, gas, weather, scope).
  7. At shift end: suspend or hand back. Leave the site safe and tidy.
  8. At job end: sign off, remove locks, return permit, confirm equipment is ready to return to service.

Handover

  • Face-to-face handover at the worksite where possible.
  • Explain status: what's isolated, what's open, what's left.
  • Incoming foreman re-signs and re-briefs the new crew.

SIMOPS

Simultaneous operations: when two permits or activities could affect each other (hot work near a line break, crane lift over a work area), the permits are cross-referenced and the issuer coordinates. Ask what else is happening nearby.
