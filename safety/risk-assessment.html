JSA & risk assessment

Writing a useful job safety analysis, the risk matrix, hierarchy of controls and last-minute risk assessments.

On this page

  2. Risk matrix (example 5 × 5)
  3. Hierarchy of controls
  4. Last-minute risk assessment (Take 5, LMRA)
  5. Common mechanical hazards to list

Job safety analysis (JSA / JHA)

  1. Break the job into steps (8–12 steps for most jobs).
  2. For each step, list the hazards (what can hurt someone).
  3. Rate the risk (likelihood × severity).
  4. List controls that reduce the risk.
  5. Assign who does each control.
  6. Review with the crew at the worksite. Update it when something changes.

Typical bands: 1–4 low, 5–9 medium, 10–16 high (needs more controls and approval), 20–25 extreme (don't start).

Hierarchy of controls

  1. Eliminate: don't do the hazardous task (pre-fabricate at ground level instead of at height).
  2. Substitute: less hazardous method or material (bolted instead of welded, water-based instead of solvent).
  3. Engineering: guards, barriers, ventilation, scaffolding with guardrails.
  4. Administrative: permits, procedures, training, signs, rotation.
  5. PPE: last line of defence.

Last-minute risk assessment (Take 5, LMRA)

At the worksite before starting and whenever something changes:

  1. Stop and think.
  2. Look for hazards around you.
  3. Assess: what could go wrong?
  4. Control: fix it before starting.
  5. Go, or stop and ask.

Common mechanical hazards to list

  • Stored energy (pressure, springs, gravity, rotation).
  • Line of fire (pinch points, falling objects, releases).
  • Manual handling of heavy parts.
  • Hot surfaces, residual product.
  • Dropped objects at height.
  • Sharp edges, grinding sparks.
  • Noise, dust, fumes.
