Chain blocks, jacking & skidding

Chain blocks and lever hoists, anchor points, hydraulic jacks, cribbing, skidding and rolling heavy equipment.

On this page
  1. Chain blocks and lever hoists
  2. Anchor points
  3. Hydraulic jacking
  4. Skidding and rolling

Chain blocks and lever hoists

  • Check WLL tag, inspection date, hook latches, chain wear and brake before use.
  • Hang from a rated anchor point (beam clamp, lifting lug, monorail trolley). Never from pipework, cable trays or handrails.
  • Pull with one person only. If one person can't pull the hand chain, it's overloaded.
  • Lever hoists (ratchet): can pull horizontally. Don't extend the lever.
  • Keep the chain straight, no twists. Don't wrap the load chain around the load.
  • Don't side load.

Anchor points

  • Must be rated and marked, or checked by an engineer.
  • Beam clamps sized for the flange width and thickness, centred.
  • Padeyes and lifting lugs welded under a qualified procedure, with NDT, and proof-load tested where required.

Hydraulic jacking

  • Jack on solid, level bearing with a steel plate on top of the cribbing.
  • Lift a little, crib a little: follow the load up with cribbing, keep gap under 25 mm.
  • Never work under a load held only by jacks. Put it on blocks.
  • Cribbing: hardwood, stacked in pairs at right angles (log cabin), height not more than twice the width of the base.
  • Use jacks with locking collars or hold valves. Hoses rated, no kinks.
  • Jack in the right places (marked jacking points) to avoid denting the equipment.

Skidding and rolling

  • Machine skates (roller dollies) under a level, rated base. Floor strong and clean.
  • Skid beams with PTFE or greased steel plates, pushed by hydraulic push-pull units or winches.
  • Control both pushing and holding back (a load on a slope can run away).
  • Turn using swivel skates or by steps.
  • Track the friction: steel on steel greased about 0.1–0.15, PTFE on stainless about 0.05–0.1 when moving, higher to start.
