Lift planning

Routine vs critical lifts, what goes in a lift plan, roles on the lift team, and the pre-lift meeting.

On this page
  1. Lift categories (typical)
  2. Lift plan contents
  3. Roles
  4. Pre-lift meeting

Lift categories (typical)

  • Routine / simple: known weight, well under capacity, standard rigging, good conditions. Generic plan or JSA.
  • Complex / critical: over 75–85% of capacity, tandem (two cranes), over live plant or occupied areas, man-riding, near power lines, blind lifts, loads of unknown weight, lifts with special rigging, very valuable loads. Written lift plan approved by an engineer.

Lift plan contents

  • Load: description, weight (and source), CoG, dimensions, lifting points.
  • Crane: type, configuration, counterweight, boom length, radius at pick and set, chart capacity, % of chart.
  • Rigging: each item with WLL, calculated loads, sling angles.
  • Ground: bearing capacity, mats, outrigger loads.
  • Site plan: crane position, swing path, obstructions, exclusion zone.
  • Weather limits.
  • Communication: signals, radios, channel.
  • Roles and names.
  • Sequence step by step, including trial lift.
  • Emergency plan.

Roles

  • Appointed person / lift planner: plans and approves.
  • Lift supervisor: in charge on site.
  • Crane operator: final say if they think the lift is unsafe.
  • Rigger / slinger: selects and attaches rigging.
  • Signaller / banksman: one person gives signals. Anyone can give STOP.
  • Tagline handlers.

Pre-lift meeting

  1. Go through the plan with everyone involved.
  2. Confirm weight, rigging and radius.
  3. Confirm the signaller and the signals.
  4. Check weather and wind.
  5. Walk the path and check the set-down area is ready (dunnage, bolts, people).
  6. Exclusion zone set and guarded.
  7. Trial lift: raise 100–300 mm, hold, check brakes, rigging, balance, then proceed.
Stop the lift if anything changes: wind rises, load weight is different from expected, a person enters the zone, or communication fails.
