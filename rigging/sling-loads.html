Sling loads & centre of gravity

Working out sling tensions at angles, unequal legs, three and four leg slings, and finding the centre of gravity.

On this page
  1. Sling angle
  2. Multi-leg slings
  3. Unequal legs (CoG off centre)

Sling angle

Tension per leg = (Load ÷ Number of legs) ÷ sin(angle from horizontal)

Quick field method: sling angle factor = sling length ÷ height (vertical distance from hook to load attachment point).

Multi-leg slings

  • Two-leg: share the load if symmetric.
  • Three and four-leg slings: assume only two legs carry the load unless the rigging is designed to equalise (spreaders, equaliser beams). Standards (e.g. EN 13414, ASME) rate 3 and 4-leg bridles on that basis or with reduced factors.

Unequal legs (CoG off centre)

For a load with its centre of gravity closer to one lifting point, the leg nearest the CoG carries more.

Vertical load on leg A = W × (distance from CoG to B) ÷ (distance A to B)

Then tension in A = vertical load on A × (sling length A ÷ height).

Example: 10 t load, lifting points 4 m apart, CoG 1 m from A. Vertical at A = 10 × 3 ÷ 4 = 7.5 t; at B = 2.5 t. The hook must be directly above the CoG, so leg A is shorter or steeper.

Finding the centre of gravity

  • Check the drawing or vendor data (CoG often marked on equipment).
  • For combined items: CoG = Σ(weight × distance) ÷ Σ weight from a reference point.
  • Trial lift: lift a few cm. The load tilts toward the CoG side. Land, adjust and retry.
  • Keep the CoG below the lifting points for stability. Loads lifted from below the CoG can flip.

D/d ratio (wire rope over a pin or shackle)

Bending a wire rope around a small diameter cuts its strength. D/d = diameter of the pin ÷ rope diameter. At D/d = 1 the eye efficiency drops to about 50%; at D/d 5 about 75%; at D/d 25 about 93%. Use large pins or protective thimbles.
