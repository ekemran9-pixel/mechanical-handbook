Small bore, threaded & tubing

Threaded pipe (NPT), small bore connections, instrument tubing and compression fittings: install it right, first time.

On this page
  1. Threaded pipe
  2. Small bore connections (SBC)
  3. Instrument tubing
  4. Two-ferrule compression fittings (Swagelok, Parker A-Lok type)
  5. Tube bending and support

Threaded pipe

  • NPT (ASME B1.20.1): 60° taper threads, 1:16 taper. Seal is in the threads, needs sealant.
  • BSPT (ISO 7/EN 10226): 55° taper. Not interchangeable with NPT.
  • BSPP (ISO 228): parallel, seals on a bonded washer or O-ring, not on the thread.
  • Hand-tight engagement plus 2–3 turns by wrench for NPT (small sizes). Don't over-tighten (cracks fittings).
  • PTFE tape: 2–3 wraps clockwise looking at the thread end, start 1 thread back. Not on the first thread, so tape doesn't get into the system. Or use anaerobic or PTFE paste sealant.
  • Seal welding of threaded joints: remove all sealant first and use a qualified procedure.

Small bore connections (SBC)

Vents, drains, instrument tappings and sample points cause many plant leaks from vibration fatigue.

  • Keep the overhung mass short: valve close to the parent pipe.
  • Brace SBCs on vibrating lines (pumps, compressors) back to the parent pipe, not to independent steel.
  • Use full-penetration weldolets or sockolets rated for the service, and 2:1 fillet profile on socket welds in vibrating service.
  • Close and plug (or cap) all vent and drain valves with a plug of the right rating.
  • Survey with the Energy Institute SBC guidelines.

Instrument tubing

  • Common: 316 SS seamless, annealed, ASTM A269. Sizes 1/4", 3/8", 1/2" OD (or 6, 10, 12 mm).
  • Wall thickness by pressure: 1/2" × 0.049" is common for impulse lines up to about 3,000+ psi; check maker's tables.
  • Don't mix inch and metric fittings and tube. Metric fittings often have a step marking on the nut.
  • Cut square with a tube cutter, deburr inside and out.

Two-ferrule compression fittings (Swagelok, Parker A-Lok type)

  1. Insert the tube fully until it bottoms in the fitting body. Nut finger tight.
  2. Mark the nut at the 6 o'clock position.
  3. Hold the body with a wrench. Tighten the nut 1¼ turns for 1/4" and larger (¾ turn for 1/16"–3/16" sizes).
  4. Check with a gap inspection gauge: the gauge must not enter the gap between nut and body (on brands that use gauges).
  5. Re-make: tighten to the original position, then about 1/4 turn more.

Never mix components from different makers. Each design is matched.

Tube bending and support

  • Use a proper bender. Minimum bend radius from the bender (often 3× OD).
  • Leave a straight length at the fitting (at least the fitting depth plus a margin) so the tube enters straight.
  • Support tubing at intervals: 1/4" every 0.9 m, 1/2" every 1.5 m (typical).
  • Slope impulse lines per hook-up drawing (liquid lines down to the transmitter, gas lines up).
