Pressure testing

Hydrostatic, pneumatic and service tests to ASME B31.3: test pressures, test packs, safety and sign-off.

On this page

  2. Test pack contents

Test pressures (ASME B31.3)

TestPressureNotes
Hydrostatic (345.4)1.5 × design pressure × (St / S)St/S = allowable stress at test temp ÷ at design temp (max 6.5). Must not exceed yield at test temperature
Pneumatic (345.5)1.1 × design pressureMax 1.33 × design for some. Hold, then reduce to design pressure for inspection
HydropneumaticAs hydrostatic
Initial service leak testOperating pressureCategory D fluid service only
Sensitive leak testLower of 1.05 bar(g) or 25% of designBubble test, Category M or specified

Hold the test pressure for at least 10 minutes, then examine all joints. On hydrotest the examination can be at design pressure if not lower than the test pressure as required.

Example: design 20 bar at 200 °C, A106 B. S at 200 °C ≈ S at ambient (138 MPa), ratio 1.0. Hydro test = 30 bar.

Test pack contents

  • P&IDs marked with test boundaries (colour highlighted), test points, vents, drains.
  • Isometrics, weld summary, NDT and PWHT clearance, punch list.
  • Test procedure, test pressure calculation.
  • Gauge calibration certificates, relief valve certificate.
  • Signatures: construction, QC, client, third party as required.

Hydrotest procedure

  1. Walk the line with the test pack. Check supports in place, spring hangers locked, welds not painted or insulated (unless allowed), all NDT complete.
  2. Remove or isolate: instruments, control valves, PSVs, rupture discs, expansion joints, check valve discs (if needed), equipment not rated for test. Fit spools and blinds rated for test pressure.
  3. Fit 2 calibrated gauges (range 1.5 to 4 × test pressure): one at the pump, one at the far or highest point. Fit a relief valve set slightly above test pressure (e.g. 5–10%).
  4. Fill from the low point with clean water (for stainless: chlorides < 50 ppm, often < 25 ppm). Vent all high points until water comes out free of air.
  5. Let temperature stabilise. Note metal temperature (avoid brittle fracture: test above MDMT + margin, typically ≥ 16 °C for carbon steel).
  6. Raise pressure gradually: 50%, hold and check, then steps of 10% to test pressure.
  7. Hold at least 10 minutes (often 30 min–1 h per spec). Record on a chart recorder if required.
  8. Inspect all joints at test or design pressure. Mark any leak.
  9. Depressurise slowly. Open vents before draining so the system doesn't pull a vacuum.
  10. Drain fully, blow dry with air where needed (especially stainless and lines going into dry service).
  11. Sign off. Reinstate removed items with new gaskets.

Pneumatic test extra rules

  • Needs owner approval and a safety assessment of stored energy and exclusion distance.
  • Leak check welds by soap solution or by sensitive method before raising pressure.
  • Raise to 25% (or 1.7 bar) first for a preliminary check, then in steps of 10% of test pressure.
  • Nobody inside the exclusion zone during pressurising and hold.
Stored energy in gas is enormous. A pneumatic test failure can throw fragments hundreds of metres. Even on hydrotests, trapped air pockets store energy. Vent thoroughly.
