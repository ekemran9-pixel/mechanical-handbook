Pipe sizes & schedules

NPS and DN, outside diameters, wall thicknesses by schedule, weights and how to identify pipe.

On this page

  3. Pipe weight

NPS and DN

  • NPS (nominal pipe size, inches) and DN (metric nominal) are names, not measurements. DN 100 = NPS 4.
  • For NPS ⅛ to 12, OD is bigger than the NPS number. From NPS 14 up, OD (in inches) = NPS.
  • All pipe of one NPS has the same OD. The schedule changes the wall and therefore the ID.
  • Carbon steel: ASME B36.10M. Stainless: ASME B36.19M (schedules with "S": 5S, 10S, 40S, 80S).

Pipe weight

Weight (kg/m) = 0.02466 × t × (OD − t) (mm, carbon steel)

Example: 6" Sch 40: 0.02466 × 7.11 × (168.3 − 7.11) = 28.26 kg/m. Stainless is about 1–2% heavier.

Mill tolerance

Seamless pipe wall can be up to 12.5% under nominal (ASTM A106/A53). Design calculations deduct it. Measure actual wall with UT where it matters.

Identifying pipe

Stencil along the pipe shows: maker, specification and grade (A106 Gr B), size and schedule, heat number, test pressure (or NH = non-hydrotested), seamless (SMLS) or welded (ERW, SAW), and dual certification if any (A106 B / A53 B / API 5L B). Keep the heat number on every cut piece (transfer it before cutting).
