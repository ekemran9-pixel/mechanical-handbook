Flange bolting (ASME PCC-1)

Bolt-up procedure, torque patterns, torque values, lubrication, tensioning, and joint integrity records.

Most flange leaks are caused by uneven or insufficient bolt load, damaged faces, wrong gaskets or misaligned flanges. A controlled bolt-up fixes most of them.

Before bolting

  1. Check flange faces for damage. Clean with a non-metallic brush.
  2. Check flange alignment (PCC-1 guide): centreline offset ≤ 1.5 mm, gap parallelism ≤ 0.8 mm difference, rotational alignment within 3 mm at bolt holes, gap no more than twice the gasket thickness. Don't force flanges together with bolts.
  3. Check gasket type, size, rating. Check stud and nut grade and length (one to three threads through the nut).
  4. Clean and lubricate threads and the nut bearing face with the specified lubricant. Note its nut factor K.
  5. Number the bolts clockwise (1, 2, 3 …).

Legacy cross-pattern sequence (PCC-1)

  1. Install: hand tighten, then snug to 20–30 N·m (not more than 20% of target). Check the gap is even.
  2. Round 1: 20–30% of target torque, cross pattern.
  3. Round 2: 50–70% of target, cross pattern.
  4. Round 3: 100% of target, cross pattern.
  5. Round 4: 100% in a circular (rotational) pattern, clockwise, until nuts no longer turn.
  6. Round 5 (optional): 100% circular after at least 4 hours (relaxation check).

Measure gaps at 4 or 8 places between rounds. Correct uneven gaps in the next round.

PCC-1 also gives faster alternative patterns (e.g. quadrant patterns) that work well on large flanges with many bolts.

Torque from bolt stress

T = K × F × D F = target stress × tensile stress area

Target bolt stress for B7 studs is often 40–70% of yield (yield 724 MPa for B7 up to 2½"). Many sites use a target of about 345–414 MPa (50–60 ksi) for spiral wound gaskets on RF flanges, adjusted for the gasket and flange limits.

Hydraulic tensioning

  • Stretches the stud directly with a hydraulic tool. Load is not affected by friction.
  • Needs longer studs (one extra diameter of thread above the nut).
  • Tension 50% or 100% of the bolts at once. With 50% coverage, do two passes plus a check pass.
  • Account for load loss when the tool is released (load transfer factor), per the calculation.

Joint integrity records

  • Tag each joint after make-up: joint ID, gasket, bolt material, torque/tension value, tool serial, date, technician, verifier.
  • Critical joints (high pressure, toxic, hot): independent verification and sign-off before leak test.
  • Bolting technicians trained and qualified (PCC-1 Appendix A).
Never tighten or loosen flange bolts on a pressurised joint (hot bolting only under a specific, approved procedure with half the bolts at a time).
