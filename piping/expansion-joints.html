Expansion joints & bellows

Metal bellows and rubber expansion joints: types, installation with shipping bars, and inspection.

On this page
  1. Types
  2. Installation
  3. Inspection

Types

  • Untied single bellows: axial movement. Needs main anchors to resist pressure thrust.
  • Tied / universal: lateral movement, tie rods carry pressure thrust.
  • Hinged and gimbal: angular movement in one or all planes.
  • Pressure balanced: absorbs axial movement without thrust on anchors.
  • Rubber (elastomer) expansion joints: water and slurry lines, pump connections. Absorb vibration and some misalignment. Need control rods where piping isn't anchored.

Installation

  • Install exactly at the installed (cold) length on the drawing. Never use bellows to take up misalignment or wrong pipe length.
  • Leave the yellow shipping bars in place until the joint is installed and all supports and anchors are complete. Remove before hydrotest or start-up (as instructed by the procedure; for some tests they stay on).
  • Flow direction arrow (for internal sleeves) must point with the flow.
  • Protect the bellows from weld spatter, arc strikes and grinding dust. A single arc strike can cause failure.
  • Pipe guides near the joint (first guide within 4 pipe diameters, second within 14 diameters) per EJMA.

Inspection

  • Bellows squirm (buckling sideways) means too much pressure or poor guiding.
  • Cracks, corrosion (chlorides on stainless bellows), dents.
  • Rubber joints: cracks, blisters, bulging. Replace typically every 5–10 years.
  • Tie rods and limit rods nuts set as drawing.
